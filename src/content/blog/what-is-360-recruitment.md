360 recruitment is a staffing model where a single recruiter owns every stage of the hiring process - from winning the client to placing the candidate and following up after the start date. Also called full-desk recruiting or 360-degree recruitment, this approach dominates small-to-mid-size agencies. It gives one person complete control over both the business development and candidate delivery sides of a placement.

Mordor Intelligence sizes the global recruiting market at [$690.3 billion in 2026](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/recruiting-market), and a significant share of that revenue flows through full-desk recruiters who manage client relationships and candidate pipelines simultaneously. Whether you're deciding between a 360 or 180 model or wondering how agency recruiters work, this guide covers the 7 stages, key trade-offs, and why AI is reshaping full-desk recruiting in 2026.

> **TL;DR:**
>
> - **One recruiter owns the whole cycle.** 360 recruiting (or full-desk recruiting) runs from business development through intake, sourcing, screening, interviews, offers, and post-placement follow-up. No handoffs, no context loss.
> - **Default model for small-to-mid agencies.** The global recruiting market is [$690.3B in 2026](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/recruiting-market) (Mordor Intelligence), much of it flowing through full-desk recruiters at sub-50-person firms.
> - **Higher revenue ceiling, higher burnout risk.** 54% of recruiters say their job got more stressful than the year before ([Employ via SHRM, 2024](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/recruiters-express-optimism-for-2025)). 180 and 270 hybrid models trade client intimacy for specialization and scale.
> - **Sourcing is the time sink.** Three hours scrolling LinkedIn is three hours not spent on client BD or closing offers. Pin scans 850M+ profiles and saves recruiters 12 hours per week on sourcing and outreach, which is why AI sourcing is reshaping the full-desk model.
> - **Agency desks carry more reqs.** On Pin, the median agency recruiter worked 2.5x as many open reqs as the median in-house recruiter (August 2025 to July 2026).
> - **Model fit depends on size and niche.** Under 15 recruiters favor 360, larger agencies with BD teams run 180 or 270, and niche firms (exec search, tech, healthcare) stick with 360 for market depth.

## What Does 360 Recruitment Actually Mean?

The 360 recruitment meaning is simple: one recruiter traces the complete circle of a placement, starting with a client need and ending with a placed, onboarded candidate. The full-desk model remains the default operating structure for thousands of small and niche agencies, which is why the term shows up so often in agency job ads.

360 recruiters serve as both salesperson and delivery specialist. On the sales side, they prospect for new clients, pitch services, negotiate fees, and maintain ongoing relationships. On the delivery side, they source candidates, screen applications, coordinate interviews, manage offers, and handle post-placement follow-up. This dual responsibility is what separates 360 from other recruiting models.

Why does it work? Relationship ownership creates accountability. When one person manages both the client and the candidate, there's no handoff gap where context gets lost. Because the recruiter who took the job brief is the same person presenting the shortlist, they understand exactly what the hiring manager wants.

Understanding the difference between [sourcing & recruiting](https://www.pin.com/blog/sourcing-vs-recruiting/) is essential here. On a full desk, you don't get to pick one - you do both, every day. That's the opportunity and the challenge.

Full-desk recruiting shows up under several names depending on the market. UK and Australian agencies default to the "360" label. In the US, you'll hear "full-desk recruiter" or "full-cycle agency recruiter." Whatever the label, the concept is identical: one recruiter, end-to-end ownership, total accountability for the placement.

Worth noting: this model is not the same as an in-house talent acquisition role. Most corporate TA teams focus on candidate-side work - sourcing, screening, interviewing, and hiring. Business development isn't part of the job because their "client" is already their employer. Fundamentally, this is an agency concept where the recruiter acts as both a salesperson and a service provider, earning revenue by placing candidates with external clients.

## 360 vs 180 Recruitment: Key Differences

Workload split is the core difference. A 360 recruiter owns both sides of the placement - client-facing business development and candidate-facing delivery. By contrast, a 180 recruiter specializes in one half. Req loads are climbing: [SHRM's 2026 Recruiting Benchmarking](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/recruiting-benchmarking) found median requisitions per recruiter rose 67% at extra-large organizations in a single year. In a 360 model, the recruiter carrying those reqs also handles the client acquisition that generates them.

| Dimension | 360 Model | 180 Model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client acquisition | Recruiter owns BD | Separate BD team |
| Job brief / intake | Recruiter-led | Handed off from BD |
| Candidate sourcing | Recruiter-led | Recruiter-led |
| Screening & shortlisting | Recruiter-led | Recruiter-led |
| Interview coordination | Recruiter-led | Recruiter-led |
| Offer negotiation | Recruiter-led | Sometimes shared with BD |
| Client relationship | Single owner | Split between BD and delivery |
| Revenue per desk | Higher ceiling | Split commission |
| Burnout risk | Higher | Lower per person |
| Scalability | Harder to scale | Easier to specialize |

### The 270-Degree Hybrid

Some agencies run a hybrid "270 model" that falls between the two extremes. A 270 recruiter handles everything except one phase - usually client acquisition. An existing BD team or account manager wins the client, then a 270 recruiter takes over from the job brief through placement. This gives firms the relationship continuity of a full-desk approach without asking one person to prospect, sell, and deliver simultaneously.

Mid-size agencies (15 to 50 recruiters) that have outgrown the full-desk model but don't want to lose client intimacy find the 270 structure particularly practical. It's also a practical stepping stone for agencies transitioning from 360 to 180 - you can move BD responsibilities to a dedicated team gradually rather than restructuring everything at once.

### Which Model Fits Your Agency?

Small agencies (under 15 recruiters) tend to favor the full-desk approach because it keeps overhead low and accountability clear. Larger agencies with dedicated sales teams often run 180 or 270 models to let recruiters focus on what they're best at. Niche firms - executive search, technical recruiting, healthcare - usually stick with 360 because deep market knowledge on both sides creates a genuine competitive edge that's hard to replicate with split teams.

## What Are the 7 Stages of the 360 Recruitment Cycle?

The 360 recruitment cycle runs through seven stages, in order:

1.  Client acquisition (business development)
2.  Job brief and intake
3.  Candidate sourcing
4.  Screening and shortlisting
5.  Interview management
6.  Offer negotiation
7.  Onboarding and follow-up

39 days: that's the median time-to-fill for nonexecutive roles in [SHRM's 2026 Recruiting Benchmarking](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/recruiting-benchmarking). Under a full-desk model, a single recruiter owns every one of those days, plus the client development work that happened before the clock started. Here's what each stage involves.

### 1\. Client Acquisition (Business Development)

Full-desk recruiters identify potential clients, reaching out through cold calls, emails, LinkedIn, or referrals, and pitch the agency's services. This stage separates the 360 model from delivery-only work. You're building a book of business, not just filling orders someone else won.

Effective BD for a full-desk recruiter means targeting companies where you already have market expertise. If you specialize in cybersecurity hiring, your prospecting list should be CISOs and security engineering managers at mid-market firms. Your revenue ceiling is determined directly by how strong your client pipeline is, so this stage never really stops - even when you're deep in delivery work on active roles.

### 2\. Job Brief and Intake

Once a client signs on, the recruiter conducts a detailed intake meeting. They learn the role requirements, team dynamics, compensation range, hiring timeline, and company culture. Because the same person who sold the client is now filling the role, there's no information loss during handoff - a persistent problem in split-desk agencies.

Rushing the intake costs time later. Full-desk recruiters who skip details end up submitting mismatched candidates, burning client trust, and extending time-to-fill. Strategic conversations, not checkbox exercises: that's how the best 360 recruiters treat intake.

### 3\. Candidate Sourcing

This is typically the most time-consuming stage. Searching databases, job boards, LinkedIn, GitHub, and internal talent pools, the recruiter builds the candidate pipeline. [Outbound sourcing](https://www.pin.com/blog/outbound-vs-inbound-recruiting/) - proactively reaching passive candidates who aren't actively job hunting - is where most full-desk recruiters spend the bulk of their daily hours.

Sourcing is also where the full-desk model shows its inefficiency most clearly. A 360 recruiter spending 3 hours searching LinkedIn is a 360 recruiter who isn't working client relationships, scheduling interviews, or closing offers. No wonder AI sourcing tools have become so popular in the full-desk world - they compress what used to be a 3-hour search into minutes.

### 4\. Screening and Shortlisting

Applications, phone screens, qualification checks, and shortlisting all happen in stage 4. Technical roles now take 17.6 interviews per hire, up 52% from 2021 ([Ashby, 2026](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/2023-recruiter-productivity-trends-report)). Screening burden keeps growing even as overall hiring volumes fluctuate.

For full-desk recruiters, screening quality directly affects your client relationship. Submit too many weak candidates, and the hiring manager loses confidence. Submit too few, and you look like you're not working the role hard enough. Finding the right balance - usually 3 to 5 qualified candidates per shortlist - is an art that experienced 360 recruiters refine over years.

### 5\. Interview Management

Scheduling coordination, prep materials, post-round feedback collection, and keeping all parties informed all fall to the 360 recruiter at this stage. Miscommunication here can kill a placement. In 2024, 26% of job seekers declined an offer after a poor experience, such as a lack of communication ([CareerPlug, 2025](https://www.careerplug.com/candidate-experience-statistics/)).

At this stage, the full-desk model's advantage shows most clearly. Because you took the original job brief, you can prep candidates with specific, insider-level detail about what the hiring manager values. You're not relaying secondhand information - you heard it directly, which makes your interview coaching more targeted and your candidates better prepared.

### 6\. Offer Negotiation

Because the full-desk recruiter knows both sides intimately, they can mediate salary expectations, start dates, benefits, and counter-offers effectively. This dual knowledge is one of the model's strongest advantages. Meanwhile, a delivery-only recruiter often lacks the client context needed to close difficult negotiations, while a BD-only rep doesn't understand the candidate's motivations.

Offer stage is also where the full-desk recruiter's relationship equity pays off. If a candidate is wavering between two opportunities, the recruiter who sourced them, screened them, and prepped them for interviews has more influence than someone the candidate just met during a handoff.

### 7\. Onboarding and Follow-Up

After the candidate starts, the 360 recruiter checks in with both the client and the new hire during the guarantee period (typically 60 to 90 days). Strong follow-up protects the placement fee, builds trust for repeat business, and turns one-time clients into long-term accounts.

Don't underestimate this stage. Many 360 recruiters skip post-placement check-ins because they're already deep into the next search. But the clients who give you repeat business are the clients who feel supported after the placement, not just during it. Fifteen-minute check-in calls at the 30-day and 60-day marks cost almost nothing and can generate thousands in future fees.

Under the full-desk model, the recruiter who won that client and closed that placement captures the full commission - there's no split with a separate business development team.

Several advantages flow from that financial alignment for agencies running full-desk teams.

**Higher per-desk revenue potential.** A full-desk recruiter who builds a strong client book and consistently delivers can generate substantial revenue. When one person controls the pipeline from prospect to placement, there's no bottleneck waiting for a BD handoff or a delivery-side recruiter to act on a warm lead.

**Deeper client relationships.** Clients work with one point of contact who understands their business, culture, and hiring patterns. This isn't just convenient - it translates directly to repeat business and referrals. Agency recruiters who manage [their own commission structures](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-agency-commission-structures/) know that client retention is worth more than any single placement fee.

**Faster decision-making.** A full-desk recruiter doesn't need to wait for approval or coordination between internal teams. If a strong candidate surfaces, they can present them to the client within hours. Speed matters in recruiting, and eliminating internal handoffs removes days from the process.

**Clear accountability.** When a placement falls through, there's no finger-pointing between BD and delivery teams. Outcome ownership lands with the 360 recruiter. Motivation to get it right the first time follows naturally.

Agencies keep roughly 3% to 8% as net margin after employment costs and overhead ([The Resource Company, 2025](https://www.theresource.com/2025/10/27/average-staffing-agency-markup-in-2025/)). In a lean full-desk operation, a productive recruiter can push past that range because there's less internal cost structure to carry.

## What Are the Biggest Challenges of Full-Desk Recruiting?

In Employ's 2024 Recruiter Nation survey of 1,200 talent pros, 54% of recruiters said their job became more stressful than the prior year ([SHRM, 2024](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/recruiters-express-optimism-for-2025)). Every stressor concentrates on a single person in the full-desk model, making burnout the most serious structural threat.

### How Many Reqs Does an Agency Recruiter Carry?

Based on Pin's data, the workload gap is structural. Across tens of thousands of job searches run on Pin between August 2025 and July 2026, the median agency recruiter worked 2.5x as many open reqs as the median in-house recruiter (5 vs 2). Half of agency recruiters carried five or more reqs over the year, compared with a third of in-house recruiters. That's the nature of a 360 desk: every client is a new req, and the recruiter who won it also has to fill it.

### Other Structural Risks

Structural risks beyond burnout affect full-desk agencies that don't plan for them.

**The single point of failure problem.** When a top 360 recruiter leaves, they take client relationships, candidate pipelines, and market knowledge with them. Agencies built on a handful of star performers face serious revenue disruption if even one walks out the door. Nobody else knows the client's hiring preferences or the candidates already in play.

**Skill dilution.** Sourcing, selling, negotiating, and account management are distinct skill sets. Asking one person to excel at all of them means most full-desk recruiters become "good enough" at everything but exceptional at nothing. Most agencies see it first in BD: the recruiter who loves sourcing lets client calls slide, and the pipeline thins out a month later.

**Scaling bottlenecks.** Growth in a full-desk agency is gated by how fast you can hire and train 360 recruiters. Since each recruiter needs both [sales skills](/blog/recruitment-vs-sales/) and delivery skills, the talent pool is smaller than for specialized 180 roles. Training takes longer too - a new full-desk recruiter typically needs 6 to 12 months before they're fully productive.

**Revenue volatility.** If a 360 recruiter has a bad BD month, their delivery pipeline dries up a few weeks later. Sales effort and delivery output are directly coupled in this model, with no buffer between them.

## When Does the 360 Model Make Sense?

The 360 model makes the most sense for solo recruiters, small agencies, and niche firms where client relationships drive repeat revenue. Whether the full-desk approach works for your situation depends on your agency size, market focus, and growth goals.

**The 360 model fits best when:**

-   **You're a solo recruiter or small agency.** If you're [starting a recruiting agency](https://www.pin.com/blog/how-to-start-recruiting-agency/), the full-desk model keeps overhead minimal and gives you direct control over revenue generation.
-   **You work a niche market.** Executive search, cybersecurity, healthcare, legal - markets where deep specialist knowledge matters more than volume. Your clients value a single expert who understands their industry inside and out.
-   **Client retention drives your revenue.** If repeat business and referrals generate most of your placements, the relationship continuity of the full-desk approach is a genuine competitive advantage that split-desk models can't replicate.
-   **You use AI tools for sourcing and outreach.** The burnout math changes completely when the most time-intensive stages are automated. A 360 recruiter with AI support operates more like a 360 recruiter with a sourcing assistant - without the payroll cost.

**Consider 180 or hybrid models when:**

-   **Your agency handles high-volume placements.** If you're filling 50+ roles per month, splitting BD and delivery lets each side operate at higher throughput without one person becoming the bottleneck.
-   **You can afford a dedicated BD team.** Once your agency reaches the size where a sales team makes financial sense, the 180 model lets recruiters focus exclusively on candidate delivery - and they'll usually produce more placements per person as a result.
-   **Your recruiters are delivery specialists.** Some recruiters are outstanding at sourcing and screening but genuinely dislike cold prospecting. Forcing them into a full-desk role means you're getting mediocre BD work from someone who could be doing exceptional delivery work.
-   **Scale matters more than per-desk revenue.** The 180 model scales more predictably because you can hire specialized talent for each function. The 360 model's revenue ceiling per desk is higher, but so is the variance.

No universally correct answer exists. Many agencies start with the full-desk model because it's simpler and more capital-efficient, then transition to 180 or hybrid structures as they grow past 20 to 30 recruiters and need more predictable throughput.

## How to Succeed as a Full-Desk Recruiter

Thriving in a 360 role takes more than working harder, especially when more than half of recruiters say the job keeps getting more stressful. Structure and systems separate the productive full-desk recruiters from the burned-out ones. Here's what makes the difference.

**Time-block BD and delivery separately.** Letting delivery work (sourcing, screening, scheduling) consume every available hour is the biggest trap for 360 recruiters. Set aside dedicated BD time each morning - even just 60 to 90 minutes - before opening your ATS or candidate pipeline. Client acquisition dries up quietly, and by the time you notice, your pipeline is already thin.

**Build systems, not just relationships.** Tracking client interactions, candidate touchpoints, and placement metrics in a CRM is non-negotiable. Memory alone isn't reliable when you're managing 15 to 20 open reqs, dozens of active candidates, and multiple client relationships. Consistent revenue comes to 360 recruiters who can see their entire pipeline at a glance.

**Specialize deeply.** Generalist 360 recruiters struggle because they're competing with specialists on both the BD and delivery sides. Pick an industry vertical, a role type, or a geography and go deep. Specialization makes your BD pitch stronger ("I've placed 40 cybersecurity engineers in the last two years") and your sourcing faster (you already know where the candidates are).

**Automate what doesn't need your judgment.** Candidate sourcing, initial outreach messages, interview scheduling, and follow-up reminders are all tasks that AI and automation tools handle well. The more of these tasks you offload, the more hours you reclaim for the stages that actually require human skill - intake conversations, candidate coaching, offer negotiations, and relationship building.

## 360 Recruitment Consultant Job Description: Duties and Skills

A 360 recruitment consultant job description combines two roles in one: agency salesperson and delivery recruiter. If you're hiring for the seat or applying for it, the duties below are what "360" signals in a job ad.

**Business development duties:**

-   Prospect and win new clients through calls, email, referrals, and networking in a chosen niche
-   Negotiate fee agreements and terms of business
-   Run intake meetings and write the job brief with the hiring manager

**Delivery duties:**

-   Source and headhunt passive candidates, then screen and shortlist them
-   Coordinate interviews, collect feedback, and prep both sides
-   Manage offers, counter-offers, and resignations through to the start date
-   Check in with the client and the placed candidate during the guarantee period

**Skills employers screen for:** a sales track record, resilience under a monthly billing target, strong phone and negotiation skills, and comfort with an ATS or recruiting CRM. In the UK, Prospects puts experienced recruitment consultant salaries at £30,000 to £33,000 before commission, with trainees starting around £20,000 ([Prospects, 2025](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/job-profiles/recruitment-consultant)).

## Which Metrics Should Every 360 Recruiter Track?

Every 360 recruiter should track three groups of numbers: business development, delivery, and financial. Output benchmarks keep rising: the average recruiter in Ashby's data went from 4.5 hires per quarter in early 2023 to 7.3 by Q1 2026 ([Ashby, 2026](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/2023-recruiter-productivity-trends-report)). For full-desk recruiters carrying both BD and delivery responsibilities, tracking the right metrics prevents you from losing visibility into pipeline health.

**Business development metrics:**

-   **New client meetings per week.** Are you building the book or coasting on existing relationships?
-   **Job orders received per month.** Tracks the direct output of your BD activity.
-   **Client retention rate.** Repeat business is the financial payoff of the full-desk model's relationship advantage.

**Delivery metrics:**

-   **Time-to-fill.** SHRM's 2026 median for nonexecutive roles is 39 days. Recruiters using Pin fill positions in an average of 14 days.
-   **Submittal-to-interview ratio.** Measures the quality of your shortlists. Low ratios mean your screening needs work.
-   **Offer acceptance rate.** Tracks how well you're managing candidate expectations and handling negotiations.
-   **Placements per quarter.** The bottom-line delivery metric. Ashby's Q1 2026 average is 7.3 hires per recruiter per quarter, a useful yardstick for the delivery half of your desk.

**Financial metrics:**

-   **Revenue per desk.** The most important number for any full-desk recruiter. Divide total billings by number of active desks.
-   **Cost-per-hire.** SHRM's 2025 benchmarking puts average nonexecutive cost-per-hire at [$5,475](https://www.shrm.org/about/press-room/shrm-releases-2025-benchmarking-reports--how-does-your-organizat). Your agency equivalent should factor in your time cost, tool subscriptions, and sourcing spend.
-   **Average fee per placement.** With direct-hire fees at 15-30% of first-year salary, a $75,000 placement generates $11,250 to $22,500 in agency revenue.

Set against an in-house team's cost-per-hire, the math shows why a productive full desk is so valuable to an agency.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is 360 recruitment?

What is 360 recruitment? In short, it's an agency staffing model where a single recruiter manages the entire hiring cycle. Starting with client acquisition and ending with post-placement follow-up, this person handles sourcing, screening, interviewing, offer negotiation, and everything in between. Also known as full-desk recruiting, it's the default structure at most agencies under 15 recruiters because it keeps overhead low while maximizing per-desk revenue potential. One person owns the client relationship and the candidate pipeline simultaneously, eliminating the context loss that happens when BD and delivery teams hand off between each other.

### What is a full desk recruiter?

A full desk recruiter is an agency recruiter who handles both halves of the job: winning and managing client accounts, and finding and placing candidates for those clients. Full desk recruitment and "360" describe the same model. The alternative is a split desk, where one person owns client development and another owns candidate delivery.

### What is the 360 hiring process?

Seven stages define the 360 hiring process: client acquisition, job brief and intake, candidate sourcing, screening and shortlisting, interview management, offer negotiation, and onboarding follow-up. Unlike split-desk models, a single recruiter owns all seven stages without handing off to a separate business development or delivery team. Median nonexecutive time-to-fill is 39 days (SHRM, 2026). Every one of those days belongs to the 360 recruiter, plus the client development work that happened before the clock started.

### What is the difference between 360 and 180 recruitment?

360 recruiters manage the full hiring cycle - client acquisition, sourcing, screening, interviews, offers, and onboarding. 180 recruiters handle only one side, typically candidate delivery, while a separate team manages business development. Agencies with fewer than 15 recruiters often run 360 because it keeps overhead low and accountability clear.

### What skills does a full-desk recruiter need?

You need sales skills (prospecting, negotiating fees, pitching), delivery skills (sourcing, screening, candidate management), and relationship management across both sides. Time management is critical since you're splitting attention between BD and delivery every single day. Recruiters who use AI tools for sourcing and outreach free up more hours for the relationship-driven stages where human judgment matters most.

### Can AI replace a 360 recruiter?

Relationship skills, negotiation ability, and market instincts define a strong full-desk recruiter - and AI can't replace any of them. What it can do is automate the most time-intensive stages: sourcing and outreach. With roughly 28% of agency recruiters in one 2025 survey saving 5 to 10 hours weekly through AI tools ([Atlas, 2025](https://recruitwithatlas.com/resources/ai-agency-recruitment-report/)), the technology acts as a force multiplier, not a replacement.

## The Bottom Line

The 360 model puts one recruiter in control of the entire hiring lifecycle - from winning the client to placing the candidate. That concentration of ownership creates strong client relationships, clear accountability, and higher per-desk revenue potential. It also creates burnout risk, scaling challenges, and single-point-of-failure exposure that agencies need to manage proactively.

Full-desk recruiters who thrive in 2026 aren't doing it the old way. Using AI to automate sourcing and outreach, they free themselves to focus on the relationship-driven stages where human judgment matters most. The model works. It just works better with the right tools behind it.
