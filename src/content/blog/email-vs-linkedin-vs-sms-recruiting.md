LinkedIn messages out-reply recruiting email 3.4x per message sent, while SMS recruiting posts the highest open rates of any outreach channel, as high as 98%. Those are the headline numbers from [Pin](https://www.pin.com)'s trailing 12 months of outreach data and from Gartner's channel research. Pin's multi-channel sequences are the reason its users see 5x better response rates than industry averages. Benchmarks from LinkedIn Talent Solutions, Gem, SHRM, and Gallup fill in the rest of the picture.

Most channel guides skip one twist: the three channels aren't legally interchangeable. Only 11% of U.S. employees are actively job hunting, while another 40% are quietly watching for openings ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/703280/worker-thriving-declines-job-market-pessimism-grows.aspx), Q4 2025). You can cold-email or cold-InMail that quiet majority. You cannot cold-text them. Under the TCPA, a recruiting text requires prior express written consent, which makes SMS a follow-up channel, not a first-touch one.

This article puts verified 2026 numbers on every channel: relative reply rates, time-to-reply medians, follow-up decay, and a matched comparison across 165,000+ candidates contacted on both email and LinkedIn. It also covers the compliance rules that decide which channel you're even allowed to use.

## Key Takeaways

- **LinkedIn wins on hit rate.** Among 165,000+ candidates contacted on both channels through Pin, LinkedIn drew 3.8x as many replies as email, and 78% of the candidates who answered at all did so only on LinkedIn.
- **Email wins on speed and volume.** The median email reply landed in under 4 hours versus 8.4 hours on LinkedIn, and email carries the bulk of outreach volume with a 57% open rate.
- **SMS recruiting wins engagement but demands consent first.** Texts reach open rates as high as 98% and response rates as high as 45% per Gartner, yet the TCPA makes cold texting passive candidates a $500 to $1,500 per-message risk.
- **Cadence beats channel.** A 4-email sequence generates 2x more replies than a single email (Gem, 2024), while Pin's data shows a fifth email touch earns barely a quarter of the replies a first touch does.
- **Pin is the best fit for teams working all three channels.** Automated email steps and AI-drafted LinkedIn and SMS tasks share one sequence, which is how its users earn 5x better response rates than industry averages.

## What Are Average Reply Rates by Recruiting Channel in 2026?

LinkedIn messages earn 3.4x the per-message reply rate of recruiting email in 2026, and SMS posts the highest response rate of the three. The LinkedIn figure comes from millions of recruiter messages sent from Pin sequences between June 2025 and May 2026. For SMS, Gartner Digital Markets research puts response rates as high as 45%, against 6% for generic email.

## The Same-Candidate Test: 165,000+ People Contacted on Both Channels

On the same 165,000+ candidates, LinkedIn out-replied email 3.8x, and candidates who answered only on LinkedIn outnumbered email-only responders 4.7x. That's original Pin research: the same people, contacted on both email and LinkedIn by the same recruiting teams between June 2025 and May 2026. Most channel benchmarks compare different audiences, which muddies the answer; this test removes that noise.

The overlap is the striking part. Of every matched candidate who replied on either channel, 78% answered only on LinkedIn, 17% only on email, and about 5% on both. A single-channel email strategy would have left nearly four out of every five responsive candidates unheard from.

That asymmetry, not any single reply rate, is the strongest argument for [multi-channel recruiting strategy](/blog/multi-channel-recruiting-strategy/) in 2026.

## Does Recruiting Cold Email Still Work in 2026?

Email remains the workhorse of passive candidate outreach. Its 2026 profile is specific: a 57% open rate across recruiter emails sent through Pin, a per-message reply rate under a third of LinkedIn's, and a median time-to-reply under 4 hours. Email replies arrived roughly 2x faster than LinkedIn replies (3.9 versus 8.4 median hours, Pin platform data).

That open-to-reply gap is the whole email game. More than half of recipients read the message; only a small fraction answer it. Two fixes carry the highest yield in Gem's benchmarks. Emails of 101 to 150 words earn peak engagement, while most recruiters write 170 to 210. Sending on behalf of the hiring manager lifts replies by 50% or more, yet only 22% of recruiters do it (Gem email benchmarks, 2024).

Follow-ups still pay, but each one pays less. Pin's data shows a clean decay curve: indexed to the first touch at 100, the second email earns 78, the third 61, the fourth 54, and the fifth just 28. Gem's research lands on the same conclusion from the other direction, with engagement flattening after the fourth email.

Front-load your best message. The decay is not subtle.

## Why Does LinkedIn Outreach Earn the Highest Reply Rate?

LinkedIn messages earned 3.4x email's per-message reply rate across outreach recruiters sent from Pin sequences between June 2025 and May 2026. LinkedIn also polices the channel, per its Recruiter help center. Recruiter and Recruiter Lite users must hold a 13% InMail response rate across 100 or more InMails in every 14-day period. Fall below it, and bulk InMail is switched off for two weeks.

What moves the LinkedIn InMail response rate most is length. In LinkedIn Talent Solutions research (2022), InMails under 400 characters earned 22% higher response than average, while messages over 1,200 characters ran 11% below it. Only 10% of InMails actually stay under 400 characters, so brevity is still an open advantage. Individually personalized InMails outperform bulk sends by about 15%, and candidates flagged "Open to Work" respond at roughly 37% higher rates.

## Is SMS Recruiting Worth It? 98% Opens, but Consent Comes First

SMS recruiting is worth it once a candidate has opted in: it delivers the highest engagement of the three channels, and it's also the most legally restricted. Gartner Digital Markets puts SMS open rates as high as 98% and response rates as high as 45%, versus 20% and 6% for email. Response speed is the channel's signature: SHRM's case study of CDW found recruiting texts answered in 3 minutes on average, with an 87% response rate and an opt-out rate 25x lower than email ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/text-recruiting-future), 2019). That's one named company's deployment rather than a population benchmark, but it shows the ceiling text message recruiting can hit.

So why isn't every recruiter texting first? Because the law says they can't.

Texting candidates requires prior express written consent under the TCPA, and that single rule decides where SMS fits in a recruiting sequence. Penalties run $500 per violation, trebled to as much as $1,500 when the violation is willful ([47 U.S.C. § 227](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227)), so a non-compliant 10,000-message campaign carries seven-figure exposure. FCC opt-out rules effective April 11, 2025 require honoring revocations within 10 business days ([BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/the-tcpas-new-opt-out-rules-take-effect-on-april-11-2025-what-does-this-mean-for-businesses.html)), and Texas's SB 140 extended state telemarketing rules to marketing texts in September 2025. One update most guides get wrong: the FCC's stricter "one-to-one consent" rule never took effect, because the 11th Circuit vacated it in January 2025 ([Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-UPDATE-11th-Circuit-Vacates-FCCs-One-to-One-TCPA-Consent-Rule)). Written consent is still mandatory.

The law, not the open rate, decides where SMS fits.

The practical playbook follows from the consent asymmetry. Cold passive candidate outreach belongs on email and LinkedIn, where no prior consent is needed. SMS enters the sequence after a candidate replies and opts in, where its 3-minute response speed makes it the strongest channel for interview scheduling, reminders, and re-engaging known candidates.

### How to Text Candidates Without a TCPA Problem

Text recruiting works when consent and context come first. A compliant flow for texting candidates looks like this:

1. **Ask in writing first.** Request a mobile number and explicit permission to text inside an email or LinkedIn reply, and keep a record of that consent.
2. **Say who you are.** Open the first text with your name, company, and the role you discussed, so it can't be mistaken for spam.
3. **Make opting out easy.** Tell candidates they can reply STOP at any time, and remove anyone who revokes consent within 10 business days.
4. **Keep texts transactional.** Use SMS for scheduling, reminders, and quick status updates, and pitch the role on email or LinkedIn.

In Pin, SMS steps sit in the same sequence as email as recruiter tasks with an AI-drafted message, so a text only goes out when the recruiter sends it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a good response rate for recruiting emails?

There's no single industry standard, but Greenhouse's own sourcing team has said it expects a 30% to 50% response rate to its initial, tightly targeted sourcing emails. Gem's analysis of 4 million recruiting emails found a 4-email sequence earns 2x the replies of a one-off send. Per individual message, expect far less, which is why cadence matters more than any single email.

### Do candidates respond better to text messages or email?

Text messages get dramatically higher engagement: open rates as high as 98% and response rates as high as 45%, versus 20% and 6% for email, per Gartner Digital Markets. The catch is consent. Recruiters need prior express written consent before texting a candidate, so SMS performs best as a follow-up and scheduling channel, not a cold-outreach channel.

### Can recruiters text candidates without consent?

No. The TCPA requires prior express written consent before recruiting texts, and violations cost $500 to $1,500 per message. Opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days under FCC rules effective April 2025. Collect a mobile number and explicit permission during email or LinkedIn conversations before moving any candidate to SMS.

### What is the average InMail response rate in 2026?

LinkedIn doesn't publish an official 2026 average, but it does set a floor: Recruiter and Recruiter Lite users must keep a 13% InMail response rate across 100 or more InMails in each 14-day window, or bulk InMail is paused for two weeks. LinkedIn messages recruiters sent from Pin sequences earned 3.4x the per-message reply rate of email between June 2025 and May 2026.

### Does LinkedIn show your InMail response rate?

Yes. LinkedIn Recruiter and Recruiter Lite both include an InMail Report that shows InMails sent, accepted, declined, and unanswered, along with your response rate. Only responses that arrive within 30 days of sending count, and the same number drives LinkedIn's 13% floor for bulk InMail.

## How to Sequence All Three Channels in One Outreach Strategy

The best-performing sequence opens with email, adds a LinkedIn touch by day 3, and saves SMS for after the candidate opts in. Reply rates held steady Monday through Thursday on both channels in Pin's data, while Saturday email replies ran about 20% below the midweek rate, so mid-week sends are the safe default. From there, a high-performing passive candidate sequence looks like this:

1. **Day 1, email.** Your strongest message goes first: the first touch carries the best reply odds you'll get, converting at over 3x the rate of a fifth touch. Keep it 101 to 150 words.
2. **Day 3, LinkedIn task.** A short message under 400 characters reaches the LinkedIn-only responders, who made up 78% of repliers in the matched test.
3. **Days 6 to 12, alternate follow-ups.** Two more touches across both channels. Gem's data shows engagement flattens after the fourth touch, so stop there instead of grinding out touch six.
4. **After the reply, SMS.** Once a candidate opts in, move scheduling and reminders to text, where responses arrive in minutes instead of hours.

Brevity is the one rule that holds across every channel. InMails under 400 characters beat the average response by 22%, 101-to-150-word emails beat 200-word essays, and SMS enforces brevity by design. Recruiters who [automate this kind of structured cadence](/blog/automated-candidate-outreach/) reclaim serious hours; Pin users report saving 12 hours per week on sourcing and outreach combined.
