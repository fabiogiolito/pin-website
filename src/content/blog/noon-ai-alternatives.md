Pin is the best AI recruiting agent for teams that want a price before a sales call, and it searches more than 850 million profiles. Noon AI (noon.ai, not the e-commerce marketplace) is an AI recruiting agent that sources candidates, runs email and SMS outreach, and books interviews through Calendly. ## What Does Noon AI Cost, and What's in the Fine Print?

Every Noon buyer gets one flat plan with "no caps, no credits, and no per-seat fees," plus a request to book a demo for the number. Fine print tells a different story. Under Noon's Terms and Conditions, effective September 14, 2026, usage beyond order-form limits is billed at "then-current standard overage rates" Noon doesn't publish. Those fees roll into your renewal, and "unlimited" handling is "subject to fair use." Help exporting your own data is billable under the same Terms. None of that appears in the five-bullet markdown summary of its Terms that Noon serves to AI assistants. By contrast, Pin lists a Free plan with one AI agent and paid plans from $99/mo. Buyers lean that way: 67% of B2B buyers prefer a buying experience with no sales rep ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience), 2026), and transparent pricing has topped buyers' wish list for vendors four years running ([TrustRadius](https://hginsights.com/news/trustradius-2026-b2b-buying-disconnect-report-reveals-ai-has-changed-how-buyers-research-but-not-what-they-trust/), 2026).

A Noon blog post from August 2026 states that "there is no published dollar price, and no credit meter to model" (Wayback Machine capture, September 4, 2026). Recruitment agencies, the same post notes, have been permitted users only since a July 2026 terms update, so agencies are brand-new Noon customers.

Instead of a price, Noon leads with a 10x guarantee: if its agents don't save recruiter time worth ten times your annual subscription, Noon refunds the subscription. According to its guarantee terms page, re-checked on September 28, 2026, four conditions apply:

- **An annual contract.** You must have signed an annual Noon subscription.
- **Weekly usage minimums.** At least one user must log in on three separate days each week and create at least one new role or search. A third month below those minimums ends the guarantee.
- **Noon's own math.** Noon estimates the time saved with a "standardized measurement methodology" it doesn't publish, values it at a flat $60 per hour, and caps the guarantee target at $1,000,000.
- **A 12-month wait.** The refund arrives after the full guarantee period. Implementation fees, professional services, usage overages, third-party fees, and separately priced add-ons aren't refunded.

The rules are also new. In an August 23, 2026 Wayback Machine capture, Noon's pricing page called the guarantee opt-in and offered to credit any shortfall in free usage. Within five weeks, that became today's refund promise.

Every condition favors the vendor: you commit for a year, keep logging in, and wait 12 months for a refund Noon calculates itself. Buyers are moving the other way: 7 in 10 software buyers say the pace of AI innovation is pushing them to ask for shorter contracts ([G2](https://company.g2.com/news/buyer-behavior-2026), 2026).

Security proof is gated, too. Neither a SOC 2 report nor a trust center is public; Noon shares security documents only under NDA. Dated September 3, 2026, its security page claims SOC 2 Type II and offers the report to enterprise buyers who sign one, and there's no public status page or subprocessor list. Support has limits as well. Noon's support policy, dated 2022, still shows its uptime promise in template brackets ("[99.9%] availability"), lets a customer designate only two support contacts, and counts its 12-hour outage response only in business hours.

Customer proof is missing, too.

Neither Capterra nor Product Hunt lists Noon, and it publishes no case study with performance numbers. The MetLife, Goldman Sachs, and Logitech tiles atop its homepage link to episodes of Noon's podcast, and its only customer quote is word-for-word from its November 2022 site, which described a different product.

## What Should an AI Recruiter Agent Do Without You?

An AI recruiter agent is software that finishes recruiting work between your check-ins: it sources candidates, sends outreach, books interviews, and learns from your decisions without a prompt for each step. Many vendors claim the label, yet Gartner estimates that only about 130 of the thousands of vendors marketing "agentic" AI offer genuinely agentic features ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027), 2025).

Six tests separate real agents from relabeled search tools:

1. **It sources continuously.** Searches re-run as profiles change.
2. **It writes and sends outreach.** Follow-ups go out without a daily nudge.
3. **It books interviews.** A reply becomes a calendar invite.
4. **It learns your bar.** Accept and reject decisions sharpen the next shortlist.
5. **It shows its work.** Every ranking has a reason, and every action lands in an audit log.
6. **It shows you the price.** You can model the cost before any sales call.

Pin passes all six. Without a published price, Noon fails the sixth before a buyer can even run the other five. Even the "fully autonomous" label strains on the second test: Noon's own [Greenhouse setup guide](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/30498665107227-Noon-integration), last updated October 2024, sends a new candidate batch every day "for your review," and emails go out only after you confirm your verdicts.

Explainability is where many other agents stumble. A 2026 iCIMS and Aptitude Research survey of 400+ U.S. talent acquisition leaders and practitioners found that 82% of companies consider AI transparency and explainability important, yet 45% lack a formal AI governance framework ([iCIMS](https://www.icims.com/company/newsroom/aiadoptionreport2026/), 2026). For the mechanics behind each step, see [how an autonomous recruiting agent works end to end](/blog/ai-recruiting-agents/).

We've noticed that the reply clock, not the first shortlist, decides whether an agent trial looks good. Across 230,000+ outreach threads in Pin's data, the median candidate replies 3.9 days after the first message, and a quarter of replies take longer than 7.9 days ([Pin's time-to-reply benchmarks](/blog/time-to-reply-interview-benchmarks/)). Sequence length matters as much as timing. In [Pin's 2026 outreach benchmarks](/blog/recruiting-outreach-benchmark-report/), the first three touches captured 93.2% of all replies, while a one-message sequence left 56% of its potential replies unclaimed. So a one-week pilot measures an agent's search while missing the follow-ups that separate one agent from another. When you trial any Noon alternative, Pin included, run it for two weeks and count interviews booked, not profiles surfaced.

## 1. Pin - Best Overall AI Recruiting Agent

If you want a more capable agent with a price you can see first, start with Pin. Pin's agent sources from 850M+ profiles aggregated from professional networks, GitHub, patents, and publications, with 100% coverage across North America and Europe. Rated 4.8/5 on G2, Pin is the highest-rated AI recruiting software on that platform.

[Pin's AI recruiting agent](https://www.pin.com/features/ai-recruiting-agent/) runs in two modes. In Assist, you approve each step. In Autopilot, it sources, ranks, drafts, sends emails, follows up, and books interviews around the clock, and you step in to close. Every ranked profile comes with a one-line reason, and the agent learns from the candidates you accept and pass on. Instead of Boolean strings, Pin's agent works from a plain-language role description: you describe the role, review the first shortlist, and it keeps re-sourcing overnight. Guardrails include approval thresholds, a full action log, a pause button, and do-not-contact lists.

According to Pin's 2026 user survey across 2,000+ organizations and 20,000+ users, recruiters accept 83% of the candidates Pin recommends into their pipelines, the highest acceptance rate in the category. They also save 12 hours a week on sourcing and outreach.

> "What I love about Pin is that it takes the critical thinking your brain already does and puts it on steroids. I can target specific company types and industries in my search and let the software handle the kind of strategic thinking I'd normally have to do on my own."
> Colleen Riccinto, Founder & President at Cyber Talent Search

**How it differs from Noon:** Email steps send automatically, and SMS and LinkedIn steps sit in the same sequence as recruiter tasks with AI-drafted messages. Scheduling syncs with your calendar natively, so there's no Calendly dependency. Pin's SOC 2 Type II status, controls, and subprocessor list are public on trust.pin.com, with the report available on request. By contrast, Noon keeps its report behind an NDA, with no public trust center.

**What it costs:** On the Free plan, you get one AI agent for one active role, with email-only sequences and 5 contact credits a week. Solo costs $99/mo on annual billing with two agents and 500 contact credits. Professional is $135 per user per month billed annually, or $179 month-to-month, with two agents per user. Business is custom-priced with unlimited seats, agents, and credits, and every email or phone lookup costs one credit ([Pin's published plans](https://www.pin.com/pricing/)).

**Trade-off:** Pin isn't an applicant tracking system. Most teams keep their ATS as the system of record and sync it through Pin's 120+ ATS integrations (an add-on on Solo and Professional). For a feature-by-feature read, see our [head-to-head Pin and Noon breakdown](/blog/pin-vs-noon/).

## Which Other Noon Alternatives Publish Prices?

Two more alternatives let you price an agent without a sales call, though their meters differ sharply. ### 2. Workable Agent - AI Sourcing Built Into an ATS

Workable announced Workable Agent in March 2026. It finds passive candidates in a pool of 400M+ profiles, screens applicants, emails candidates, and invites them to screening calls, all inside Workable's applicant tracking system. Credits power every action: 2 per passive candidate identified, 1 per screened candidate, and 10 per candidate chat interaction. Paid accounts start with 3,000 free credits, packs begin at 5,000 credits for $600, and credits expire after a year with no refunds. The Standard plan lists at $299/mo billed annually ($3,588 a year) for companies with 1-20 employees ([Workable pricing page, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20260917170809/https://www.workable.com/pricing), re-checked September 28, 2026).

**Good for:** Teams already running Workable. At $0.12 a credit, each sourced candidate costs about $0.24, which is predictable. However, the agent only works inside Workable, and the free trial doesn't include it. Our breakdown of [Workable's full plan pricing](/blog/workable-pricing/) covers every tier.

### 3. Indeed Sourcing Assistant - An Agent Inside Indeed's Resume Pool

Indeed added Sourcing Assistant to its Smart Sourcing subscription in June 2026 ([HR Brew](https://www.hr-brew.com/stories/indeeds-new-ai-assistant-takes-aim-at-sourcing), 2026). Once you assign a job, it discovers candidates and sends personalized outreach around the clock, with up to three jobs running at once. Indeed says candidates it surfaces are 2.9x more likely to be hired, and hired 31% faster, than those from other channels, which is a company-reported figure ([Indeed Smart Sourcing page, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20260920191043/https://www.indeed.com/employers/smart-sourcing), re-checked September 28, 2026). Smart Sourcing Professional costs $520 a month, or $4,992 a year, and includes 100 candidate contacts per month with a six-month rollover. Each extra contact costs $5.20, and a 14-day free Smart Sourcing trial is available.

**Good for:** Teams that already post and hire on Indeed. The contact meter is the constraint: at $5.20 per extra contact, a 300-candidate outreach month adds $1,040 on top of the plan. Anyone budgeting beyond Smart Sourcing can check [how Indeed prices its employer products](/blog/indeed-pricing/).

## Which Noon AI Competitors Require a Sales Call?

Gem, LinkedIn Hiring Assistant, Leoforce, and Paradox share Noon's biggest drawback: you'll need a sales call to learn the price. In exchange, they bring bigger networks or bundled platforms, with prices to match. ### 4. Gem - AI Sourcing Agent Inside an ATS and CRM Suite

Gem's AI Sourcing Agent searches 800M+ profiles and surfaces best-fit candidates inside Gem's recruiting CRM, which also bundles analytics, scheduling, and an ATS. Both main plans, Gem + Your ATS and Gem All-in-One, are custom-priced by company headcount. Only Gem's startup program, for companies under 100 employees, lists rates, starting at $130 a month billed yearly for 1-10 employees ([Gem pricing page, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20260918194321/https://www.gem.com/pricing), re-checked September 28, 2026).

**Good for:** Mid-market teams consolidating their CRM and ATS in one purchase. Because pricing scales with company headcount rather than usage, you buy a platform to get the agent, whereas Pin sells the agent on its own, starting with a free plan. Vendor contract data appears in [what Gem's contracts actually run](/blog/gem-pricing/).

Hiring Assistant is an add-on to LinkedIn Recruiter, not Recruiter Lite, and LinkedIn publishes no price for either.

**Good for:** Teams already paying for Recruiter seats who want an agent inside the same workflow. The agent searches only LinkedIn's network, and the add-on price isn't public. Its homepage, re-checked September 28, 2026, pitches outcome pricing: "If a candidate does not qualify, you do not pay." For clients who want it, a human recruiter validates each candidate first. Pricing is quote-only.

**Good for:** Buyers who'd rather pay per qualified candidate than per seat. The mix leans toward inbound and rediscovery rather than outbound sourcing, and without published rates you can't compare its per-candidate cost with anything else in this guide.

Paradox publishes no price list ([Paradox homepage, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20260831191852/https://www.paradox.ai/), 2026).

**Good for:** Hourly hiring where inbound volume, not outbound sourcing, is the bottleneck. Budget extra time for security review. ## How to Run a Two-Week Bake-Off Before You Sign

A fair test of two or three agents takes about two weeks, since replies keep arriving long after the first message. Run it in five steps:

1. **Pick two live roles.** Choose one hard-to-fill role and one repeatable role, and give every agent the same intake notes.
2. **Turn on follow-ups.** Run at least three touches per candidate, since most replies arrive by the third.
3. **Count booked interviews.** Track interviews and hires, not profiles surfaced or emails sent.
4. **Price the real scenario.** Multiply seats, agents, credits, and contact allowances by the number of roles you'll actually run.
5. **Ask for the paperwork.** Request the [SOC 2 report](/blog/soc-2-recruiting-software/) and the contract term before the trial ends, not after.

Signing with Noon means an annual commitment before it shows you a price, a trust center, or a track record. The Noon AI alternatives above publish more of what you need to decide.
