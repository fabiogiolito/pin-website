## What Do Users Like About Pin?

Praise in Pin's reviews clusters around four themes: AI search that finds people standard searches miss, outreach that turns into placements, a short learning curve, and speed to hire.

Filters come up again and again. Reviewers like describing a role in plain language and then spelling out exclusions, instead of stacking Boolean strings. For specialist recruiters, that precision is the point. Laura Rust, Founder and Principal at Rust Search, describes it this way:

> "Pin helps me find needle-in-a-haystack candidates with real precision, like filtering by company size during someone's tenure, so I can zero in on the right operators for a specific stage."

Recruiters leaving LinkedIn Recruiter make a related point about reach. Ryan Levy, Managing Partner at Cruit Group, said Pin "gave us the ability to find candidates that didn't appear on LinkedIn Recruiter."

Agency recruiters who tie Pin to revenue give the loudest praise. Nick Poloni, President at Cascadia Search Group, wrote:

Pin reports 5x better response rates on its multi-channel sequences than industry averages. ### A short learning curve for small teams

### Speed to hire

Time-to-fill is where in-house founders notice the difference first. Fahad Hassan, CEO and Co-founder at Range, reported hiring a software engineer and a financial planner "within just two weeks of using the product." Averaged across Pin's 2026 user survey of 2,000+ organizations and 20,000+ users, time-to-fill lands at 14 days.

### Why the same search draws praise and complaints

After shipping to thousands of recruiting teams, we've learned that most praise and most complaints trace back to one design choice: Pin is precision-first. Instead of returning 20 pages of loose matches, it stack-ranks a shortlist, so a recruiter who describes the role in detail gets a tight list worth contacting. Someone expecting Boolean-style exhaustiveness can read that same shortlist as "missing people," which is why the fix lives in the brief rather than the database. Describe outcomes, not just titles: the company's stage, the scope of the team, and the tools that matter. Reject weak matches with a reason, because that feedback reshapes the next batch. Since September 2026, one more question has joined the usual ones about trials and ATS connectors: whether LinkedIn's removal of Pin's company page changes anything. It doesn't, and we explain why below.

### "It misses candidates I found on my own"

A few G2 reviewers said Pin sometimes surfaced the wrong profiles, and one wrote that rejected profiles came back after feedback.

The brief is the lever.

Pin's precision-first ranking returns a tight shortlist rather than pages of loose matches, so the detail you put in shapes who appears. The practical test: rerun a search for a role you already filled and check whether your eventual hire shows up. Write the brief in plain language, add exclusions, and reject with reasons.

### "Coverage is thinner outside tech"

Two reviewers named construction as a weak spot, and one said Pin does better with tech roles. That tracks with where the data comes from: professional networks, GitHub, Stack Overflow, patents, and publications, where software, product, and professional talent leaves a rich trail. Pin covers 100% of candidates in North America and Europe, and its profiles run deepest for those roles. If you're hiring mostly field trades, run a free-plan search on a live role before you buy.

### "Some contact data bounces"

Two mid-market reviewers flagged emails that bounced or phone numbers that were wrong. Contact data decays everywhere because people keep moving. Median job tenure was 4.1 years in January 2026, according to the [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/tenure.nr0.htm), and U.S. employers made roughly 5 million hires a month through mid-2026 ([BLS JOLTS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm)). Verify before you sequence, and prefer personal email for long-horizon passive candidates. Pin prices personal email, work email, and phone lookups at 1 credit each, so trying a second channel stays cheap.

### "My ATS isn't connected"

Pin connects to 120+ ATSs, and reviewers running a few agency systems asked for theirs. ATS sync is included on Business and available as an add-on on Solo and Professional. For systems not yet on the list, CSV and PDF exports bridge the gap.

### "The app had bugs"

A few reviews from late 2025 mentioned bugs, such as a campaign that froze on duplication. None of the G2 reviews from April 2026 repeats them. As with any sequencing tool, confirm the stop rules during your trial.

## How Should You Read an AI Recruiting Tool Review?

Read any AI recruiting tool review with five checks: the review label, the rules, the reviewer's team size, the date, and a test search of your own. In that TrustRadius report, 74% of B2B technology buyers used reviews to inform a purchase. [G2's April 2026 research](https://company.g2.com/news/g2-research-the-answer-economy) found 51% of B2B software buyers now start research in an AI chatbot more often than in Google. By July, review sites had overtaken chatbots as the top source shaping shortlists, 38% to 37%, in the [G2 2026 Buyer Behavior Report](https://company.g2.com/news/buyer-behavior-2026).

Reading pin.com reviews well takes the same discipline as reading reviews of any recruiting software. HR software is the third most common source of purchase regret, according to [Capterra's 2025 Tech Trends survey](https://www.capterra.com/resources/tech-trends-hr-software-purchase-regret/), and successful software buyers were 52% more likely to factor in review and comparison sites.

1. **Check the review label.** G2 marks whether each review was organic, invited by G2, or incentivized.
2. **Know the rules.** The [FTC's rule on fake reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), in force since October 21, 2024, bans fake reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, and company-controlled "independent" review sites. Civil penalties reach $53,088 per violation, and the FTC [sent warning letters](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-warns-10-companies-about-possible-violations-agencys-new-consumer-review-rule) under the rule to 10 companies in December 2025.
3. **Match the reviewer to you.** Give the most weight to reviews from teams your size, hiring the kinds of roles you hire.
4. **Look at dates.** A complaint from 2025 may describe a product that has since changed.
5. **Test on a filled role.** Rerun a search for someone you already hired and see who each tool surfaces.
