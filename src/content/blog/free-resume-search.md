# Free Resume Search: 8 Sites to Find Candidates (2026)

The best free resume search in 2026 means stacking three or four sites instead of one: Indeed Resume (225M+ resumes), GitHub (180M+ developers), Google X-Ray on public LinkedIn profiles, and a niche channel like Wellfound for startups or Craigslist for local hires. For teams that outgrow free, [Pin](https://www.pin.com) is the most accessible full-platform AI recruiter, with 850M+ candidate profiles from $100/mo and a free tier that needs no credit card. None of the eight solve the whole funnel alone, and most gate candidate contact behind a paid plan. Together, though, they cover the majority of roles a recruiter sourcing on a tight budget will face, and three let you find resumes and message candidates at zero cost.

This article walks through the 8 sites worth using, what's actually free vs. what's paywalled, and which ones work as a CV database outside the US. It also pins down the moment when stitching free tools costs more recruiter hours than a real platform would cost in dollars.

## Why Are Recruiters Going Free in 2026?

Non-executive hires now average $5,475 and 44 days to fill, up 24% since 2021 (<a href="https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/2025-recruiting-benchmarking" rel="nofollow">SHRM 2025 Recruiting Benchmarking Report</a>). Executive hires run $35,879. Those numbers explain why "free resume search" is one of the fastest-growing recruiter queries even as overall recruiting tech spend is rising.

Two forces are reshaping how recruiters think about budget in 2026. The first is AI: 43% of HR teams now use AI in some form, up from 26% in 2024, and 51% of those teams use it specifically for recruiting (<a href="https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/2025-talent-trends/ai-in-hr" rel="nofollow">SHRM 2025 Talent Trends: AI in HR</a>). The second is cost discipline. Gartner described 2026 talent acquisition as being driven by exactly two things: "the AI revolution and the need to drive cost out of the business" (<a href="https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-07-gartner-says-ai-revolution-and-cost-pressures-are-two-forces-driving-the-top-four-trends-for-talent-acquisition-in-2026" rel="nofollow">Gartner, October 2025</a>).

Layer in a tight labor market, with 6.9 million U.S. job openings still on the books in February 2026 (<a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.htm" rel="nofollow">BLS JOLTS</a>). The math gets brutal: more roles to fill, longer fills, higher per-hire costs, but boards and CFOs asking for less spend. A free resume database isn't a long-term sourcing strategy, and knowing where to find free resumes is only step one. It's a way to free up budget so the spend that does happen lands on tools that move the needle, like our [free AI recruiting tools](/blog/free-ai-recruiting-tools/) roundup covers in detail.

> **The short version:**
>
> - **8 sites are worth bookmarking to find resumes free.** Four general databases (Indeed, Wellfound, PostJobFree, JobSpider) and four specialized channels (Jobvertise, Google X-Ray, GitHub, Craigslist), two of which double as a free CV database outside the US.
> - **"Free" usually means free to search, not free to contact.** Indeed, Wellfound, and PostJobFree all gate candidate contact behind paid plans. JobSpider, GitHub, and Craigslist genuinely are free end-to-end.
> - **GitHub is the cheapest channel for engineering hires, but not a complete one.** Across a sample of 500,000+ software engineers drawn from Pin's index, only 22.2% have a public GitHub profile a recruiter can tie to a real person, so treat it as a supplement rather than a single source.
> - **The Jobvertise free tier caps you at 3 resumes per day.** Most articles bury this; it's a dealbreaker for active sourcing.
> - **Pin starts at $100/mo with a free tier**, which is the bridge most cost-conscious teams take when stitching free tools eats more hours than a real platform would cost.

## What Does "Free" Actually Mean in Resume Search?

Across the 8 sites in this guide, only 3 are genuinely free end-to-end (JobSpider, GitHub, Craigslist). Most articles on this topic skip the part that matters most to recruiters: free to search is not the same as free to contact. Indeed, Wellfound, PostJobFree, and Jobvertise all let you browse and filter candidates at no cost, then charge for the message that actually starts the conversation. That's the trap. Every resume search engine for employers on this list is free in one narrow sense: you can search resumes without paying. Almost none are free in the sense that matters, which is reaching the person behind one. You spend an afternoon shortlisting 40 promising candidates on Indeed, then realize messaging them costs $400/mo for the Professional plan.

There are three honest categories among the 8 sites in this guide:

1. **Free to search, paid to contact** (Indeed, Wellfound, PostJobFree, Jobvertise). Useful for shortlisting and second-stage candidate research. Plan for a paid contact tier or pair with a separate email finder.
2. **Free end-to-end** (JobSpider, GitHub, Craigslist). Search and contact are both free. Quality and pool size vary, but the dollar spend is genuinely zero.
3. **Free with caveats** (Google X-Ray Search). The search itself is free and surfaces real LinkedIn profiles, but contacting those candidates still routes through LinkedIn, which means an InMail or a connection request, or pulling the email out via a separate finder tool. Our guide to the [best email finder tools for recruiters](/blog/best-email-finder-tools-recruiters/) covers what plugs into this gap.

Knowing which category each site falls into changes which one you reach for first. If you have a contact-heavy week, lead with JobSpider, GitHub, and Craigslist. If you're shortlisting for a passive search, Indeed and Wellfound are better starting points even with the contact paywall. No single resume finder covers all three categories, which is why experienced sourcers keep two or three tabs open rather than hunting for one perfect site.

## Which Free Resume Databases Should Recruiters Use First?

Four general-purpose databases hold a combined 238M+ resumes between them: Indeed (225M+), Wellfound (10M startup-ready), PostJobFree (~1M), and JobSpider ("thousands"). Each works as a broad resume database for recruiters who need coverage before they need precision, and the filters will feel familiar from any ATS. Any role at any geography or experience level can usually be found in one of the four.

### 1. Indeed Resume Search (Smart Sourcing)

**Good for:** Almost any role at any company size. The first place most recruiters check.

![Screenshot of the Indeed Smart Sourcing employer page](/images/urlbox/web-archive-org-e8281110.webp)

With 225 million resumes across 60 countries and 28 languages, Indeed runs the largest free-to-search candidate pool on the open web (<a href="https://www.indeed.com/employers/smart-sourcing" rel="nofollow">Indeed Smart Sourcing</a>). You can browse and filter by job title, skills, location, last-active date, education, and several other dimensions without an account.

The catch: contacting candidates requires a Smart Sourcing subscription. Standard is $120/mo for 30 contacts. Professional is $400/mo for 100 contacts (<a href="https://www.saasworthy.com/product/indeed-resume/pricing" rel="nofollow">SAASworthy</a>). Trials run 14 days on that paid tier. Smart Sourcing's AI-matching layer claims sourced candidates are 31% faster to hire and 2.9x more likely to be hired than other sourced candidates, though those numbers come from Indeed's own product page.

**Downside:** The 200M+ figure has been repeated since at least 2021 with no recent date stamp. Active resume freshness varies. The contact paywall is steep for high-volume sourcing, and the candidate pool skews toward active job-seekers, which is the opposite of what most senior or specialist searches need.

### 2. Wellfound (formerly AngelList Talent)

**Good for:** Hiring at seed-to-Series B startups, especially in tech.

![Screenshot of the Wellfound Recruit homepage](/images/urlbox/wellfound-com-6ceaab7b.webp)

Wellfound has 10 million startup-ready candidates who have explicitly opted into startup-stage employers (<a href="https://wellfound.com/recruit/all-features/find-talent" rel="nofollow">Wellfound Recruit</a>). The free tier covers job posting, a lightweight ATS, basic candidate search, and free messaging to inbound applicants. That last detail matters: free messaging on Wellfound applies to candidates who applied to your job, not to proactively reaching passive candidates.

[Recruit Pro](/blog/recruit-angellist/) at $499/mo unlocks advanced filters, full resume access, personalized pitch templates, instant scheduling, and unlimited proactive messaging. Wellfound also runs a separate AI-sourcing product (Autopilot) that claims to scan 500M+ profiles, but that's a different tier and a different price point.

**Downside:** Coverage outside tech and startups is thin. The free tier's "limited reach" on candidate search isn't quantified publicly, and $499/mo Recruit Pro is steep for solo agencies or one-off hires.

### 3. PostJobFree

**Good for:** Budget-conscious SMEs running broad, lightweight sourcing.

![Screenshot of the PostJobFree homepage](/images/urlbox/web-archive-org-b9170171.webp)

<a href="https://www.postjobfree.com/" rel="nofollow">PostJobFree</a>'s free tier covers unlimited resume search and unlimited free job postings, with distribution to a network of partner job boards thrown in. Third-party reviews put the database at roughly 1 million resumes. Its live counter shows a much smaller active pool at any given moment, so the 1M+ figure is best read as a cumulative total rather than a current snapshot.

Contacting candidates and viewing full profiles requires a paid plan, ranging from $29/mo for basics up to $399/mo for 100 promoted jobs and 1,000 resume contacts.

**Downside:** Active resume volume is modest, the UI feels dated, and there's no documented Boolean search support. Use PostJobFree as a secondary check rather than a primary database.

### 4. JobSpider

**Good for:** Local and regional U.S. and Canada hiring on a zero budget, especially blue-collar and trades.

![Screenshot of the JobSpider homepage](/images/urlbox/jobspider-com-bf50a1d2.webp)

JobSpider is the rarest thing in this category: completely free, end-to-end. Job posting is free. Resume search is free. Contacting candidates via the on-site web form is free. There's no upsell path, no contact paywall, no per-day cap. The pool is described as "thousands of active job seekers" with no specific number disclosed, so it's much smaller than Indeed or Wellfound.

Expect the trade-offs of a site that has barely changed since 2003: no mobile optimization, no ATS integration, no analytics, and no AI-driven matching. Manual web-form contact only.

**Downside:** Smallest pool of the eight sites. Better for a specific local search than for an ongoing sourcing pipeline.

## Which Specialized Free Resume Channels Work Best?

Four specialized free channels reach 180M+ developers (GitHub) plus an estimated 1B+ public LinkedIn profiles (via Google X-Ray) plus international and local pools that the general databases miss. They're narrower in scope but deeper in their lane. Skip them for the wrong role and you'll waste an afternoon. Use them for the right role and they outperform the general databases.

### 5. Jobvertise

**Good for:** International sourcing on zero budget, especially UK, India, Singapore, Australia, and Germany.

![Screenshot of the Jobvertise homepage](/images/urlbox/jobvertise-com-39796373.webp)

<a href="https://www.jobvertise.com/" rel="nofollow">Jobvertise</a> calls itself "the world's largest free jobs and resume database" and claims 2 million resumes with about 20,000 added monthly. International coverage is the differentiator. Search, alerts, and viewing recently posted resumes are all free. Free job posting is also included.

The honest catch most articles bury: the free tier caps you at viewing 3 resumes per day. That's effectively unusable for active sourcing on a single search. Paid plans run from $32.99/mo (Entry) to $99.99/mo (Pro) and remove the cap. If you have a few hours a week and a long timeline, the free cap is workable. For anything time-sensitive, plan to upgrade or use Jobvertise as a Tier 2 source.

**Downside:** The 3-per-day cap, plus a dated UI and a "world's largest free" marketing claim that's hard to verify against Indeed or Wellfound.

### 6. Google X-Ray Search

**Good for:** Surfacing public LinkedIn profiles without a LinkedIn Recruiter seat. Especially useful for senior, technical, and niche roles.

![Screenshot of the Google search homepage used as the X-ray search starting point](/images/urlbox/google-com-595c3cce.webp)

X-ray search uses Google to find pages on a specific domain. Recruiters lean on one classic pattern: `site:linkedin.com/in "[job title]" "[skill]" "[city]"` surfaces LinkedIn profiles matching a search. It works on GitHub, personal portfolios, conference speaker pages, Stack Overflow, and any other site with public profile pages.

Two important caveats. First, contact is still gated. X-ray surfaces the profile, not the email. Connecting through LinkedIn requires either a free connection request (slow, low yield) or a paid InMail. Most recruiters pair X-ray with a separate email finder. Second, LinkedIn has progressively restricted Google's ability to index full profile pages since 2022. X-ray is still viable, but the yield has declined, and many results now require a LinkedIn login to see the full profile. Technical roles are where the technique still earns its keep, since GitHub and portfolio sites surface alongside LinkedIn results.

**Downside:** Declining yield on LinkedIn-only searches. Time-intensive without Boolean fluency. No contact data extraction.

### 7. GitHub

**Good for:** Engineers, data scientists, ML/AI talent. The single best free channel for technical hires.

![Screenshot of the GitHub homepage](/images/urlbox/github-com-d7b3438d.webp)

GitHub crossed 180 million developers in 2025, with 36 million joining in that year alone, including 28 million in the U.S. and 21.9 million in India (<a href="https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/" rel="nofollow">GitHub Octoverse 2025</a>). Profiles are public and searchable by language, location, follower count, repository activity, and commit history. All of it is free.

GitHub's case gets stronger read alongside the Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey: 46% of developers say they're not currently looking for a new job (<a href="https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/" rel="nofollow">Stack Overflow 2025</a>). That's a passive talent pool of roughly 80+ million developers who will never apply to a job board posting. GitHub is the only free channel that reaches them at scale, which is why it's covered in detail in our [GitHub recruiting guide](/blog/github-recruiting/).

One ceiling is worth knowing before you lean on GitHub too hard. Across a sample of 500,000+ software engineers drawn from Pin's index, only 22.2% have a public GitHub profile a recruiter can tie back to a real, contactable person. Adding the Stack Overflow family and dev.to barely moves that figure, to 22.9%, because those communities surface largely the same engineers rather than new ones. Sourcing from GitHub alone therefore leaves roughly four in five engineers invisible. That is the clearest argument in this guide for stacking channels instead of betting everything on one.

**Downside:** Contact info is inconsistent. Some profiles list an email, some hide it, some embed it in commit metadata. You'll need a separate email finder for many candidates. Not useful for non-technical roles.

### 8. Craigslist

**Good for:** Hourly, blue-collar, local service roles. Trades, food service, retail, event staffing, hourly admin.

![Screenshot of the Craigslist sites directory page](/images/urlbox/craigslist-org-6e7875e2.webp)

The <a href="https://www.craigslist.org/about/help/employment_jobs" rel="nofollow">Craigslist</a> resume section is genuinely free for recruiters. Browsing is free, search by keyword and availability is free, and contacting candidates via the anonymous email relay is free. Only one piece costs money: posting a JOB listing runs $10 to $75 depending on city. Searching and contacting candidates who have posted resumes themselves doesn't cost anything.

Craigslist still delivers for local hourly roles across major U.S. metros: NYC, LA, SF, Chicago, DC, and Seattle. Quality varies wildly by city and category. For non-traditional channels worth pairing with Craigslist, our guide on [sourcing talent on Reddit](/blog/source-talent-reddit/) walks through another zero-cost option that some teams find more reliable for niche communities.

**Downside:** Spam, fake listings, no identity verification, no resume standardization, no ATS integration, and no mobile app. Not a fit for professional or technical roles.

## Truly Free vs. Gated: The 8 Sites Compared

Paid contact tiers across these eight sites span $29/mo to $499/mo, while three of them cost nothing end to end. Side-by-side, the free vs. gated picture is much clearer than any of the 8 sites' own marketing pages let on.

| Site | Resume Pool | Search Free? | Contact Free? | Starting Paid Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indeed Resume | 225M+ | Yes | No | $120/mo (Standard) |
| Wellfound | 10M+ | Limited | Inbound applicants only | $499/mo (Recruit Pro) |
| PostJobFree | ~1M | Yes | No | $29/mo |
| JobSpider | "Thousands" | Yes | Yes | Free |
| Jobvertise | ~2M | Capped at 3/day | Capped at 3/day | $32.99/mo |
| Google X-Ray | LinkedIn 1B+ surface | Yes | Only via LinkedIn | $0 (X-ray) + InMail |
| GitHub | 180M+ developers | Yes | Only if email is public | Free |
| Craigslist | Local, undisclosed | Yes | Yes | Free (search side) |
| **LinkedIn Recruiter Corp** | **1B+** | **No, paid only** | **No, paid only** | **~$10,800-$15,000/yr** |
| **Pin** | **850M+** | **Yes, free tier** | **Yes, within plan limits** | **Free tier, then $100/mo** |

Pricing verified September 2026.

The headline takeaway: only three of the 8 free sites (JobSpider, GitHub, Craigslist) are genuinely free end-to-end. Everything else gates the part of the workflow that actually matters: getting a message in front of the candidate.

## Which Free CV Databases Work Outside the US?

Three of the eight sites double as a free CV database for non-US hiring: Jobvertise, Google X-Ray, and GitHub. Recruiters outside North America search for "CV" rather than "resume," and that one word changes which tools return usable results.

Jobvertise comes closest to a dedicated international CV finder here, with resumes concentrated in the UK, India, Singapore, Australia, and Germany. Its 3-per-day free cap applies exactly as it does for US searches. Google X-Ray is the more flexible route. Swapping the operator to `site:uk.linkedin.com/in "[job title]" "[city]"` or pointing it at another country subdomain surfaces profiles the US-centric databases never index at all. GitHub, meanwhile, is geography-agnostic by design, and with 21.9 million developers in India alone it is frequently the strongest channel for technical hiring across APAC.

None of them amount to a genuine one-stop CV search engine. Indeed runs separate country domains with separate resume pools, PostJobFree and JobSpider are effectively US and Canada only, and Craigslist coverage thins out fast beyond the major American metros. A free resume search that works in London or Bangalore looks nothing like one that works in Chicago. Sustained international sourcing usually settles into X-Ray plus a paid platform whose index isn't partitioned by country.

## Free Resume Search: Frequently Asked Questions

### Can recruiters search resumes for free?

Yes. Indeed Resume, Wellfound, PostJobFree, JobSpider, Jobvertise, GitHub, and Craigslist all let recruiters search candidates without a paid subscription. The catch is that contacting those candidates is gated on most of the major platforms (Indeed, Wellfound, PostJobFree, Jobvertise). JobSpider, GitHub, and Craigslist are the three sites where search and contact are both free.

### Which is the best free resume database for employers?

Breadth goes to Indeed Resume Search, with 225M+ resumes across 60 countries (<a href="https://www.indeed.com/employers/smart-sourcing" rel="nofollow">Indeed</a>). For technical hires, GitHub is the best free database with 180M+ developers and the only free way to reach the 46% of developers who aren't actively job-hunting (<a href="https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/" rel="nofollow">Stack Overflow 2025</a>). Most recruiters use both rather than picking one.

### How do I find candidate resumes without paying for LinkedIn Recruiter?

Use Google X-Ray Search to surface public LinkedIn profiles with `site:linkedin.com/in "[job title]" "[city]"`, layer Indeed Resume for U.S. and global breadth, and add GitHub for technical roles. For ongoing sourcing at any volume, Pin's free tier covers the workflow LinkedIn Recruiter charges $10,800+ per year for, with a full $100/mo Starter plan beyond that.

### What's the best free resume search engine for tech recruiters?

GitHub. Profiles are searchable by programming language, location, repository activity, and commit history, and the platform reached 180M+ developers in 2025 (<a href="https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/" rel="nofollow">GitHub Octoverse 2025</a>). Pair it with Google X-Ray on Stack Overflow and personal portfolio sites to triangulate contact info, since GitHub email visibility is inconsistent.

### Is Indeed Resume Search free for employers?

Searching is free without an account; contacting candidates requires a Smart Sourcing subscription starting at $120/mo for 30 contacts (Standard) or $400/mo for 100 contacts (Professional), with a 14-day trial available (<a href="https://www.saasworthy.com/product/indeed-resume/pricing" rel="nofollow">SAASworthy</a>). The "free" label only covers the search and shortlisting portion of the workflow.
