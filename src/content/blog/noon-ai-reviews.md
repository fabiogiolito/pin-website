## What Is Noon AI?

Noon AI is an AI recruiting agent from Portal Inc., a New York company, that searches the open web for candidates and contacts them through email and SMS sequences. On its product page, Noon says it searches "across the whole web, not just LinkedIn." Noon calls the agent autonomous, but its own Greenhouse setup guide tells a different story. Last updated October 2024, that [Noon integration guide](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/30498665107227-Noon-integration) opens with "Noon AI is a fully autonomous AI talent sourcer." Then it describes a new batch of candidates arriving every day "for your review," with emails sent only after you confirm your verdicts. Your team calibrates each role's hiring bar with thumbs-up and thumbs-down feedback, and results sync to your applicant tracking system. That calibration takes time: Noon's own buyer's guide (June 2026 capture) says match accuracy "typically reaches 70-80%+ positive signal rates" only after 20-30 reviews. Searches for Noon recruiting also surface noon.com, a Middle East e-commerce marketplace, and an unrelated AI design startup; this review covers noon.ai only.

Sourcing is only part of the Noon AI recruiting lineup now. Today, Noon sells a set of agents on a single plan, led by an AI Sourcer, AI Outreach, and an AI Scheduler that books interviews through Calendly. Agencies are brand-new territory: Noon's own blog says recruitment agencies have been "permitted users since the July 2026 terms update."

What Noon leaves out is just as telling. Its About page names no founders; Raymond Guo is publicly identified as a co-founder in his HackerNoon author bio. Search results also blur two companies: the $44M April 2026 seed sometimes credited to "Noon" belongs to a different company, an AI product-design tool.

The pricing page skips the fine print, but Noon's Terms and Conditions, effective September 14, 2026, fill in clauses Pin's terms don't carry:

- Usage beyond order-form limits is billed at Portal's "then-current standard overage rates," which Noon doesn't publish, and your renewal "shall include the additional fees for such excess users and usage."
- The "unlimited" inbound and outbound handling "is subject to fair use."
- Any help exporting your own data "shall be billable at Portal's standard rates."
- You indemnify Noon against claims, including claims by candidates, tied to your hiring: the customer "assumes all risks arising from Customer's hiring, recruiting, placement, and employment activities."

Those clauses contradict a plan sold as "no caps, no credits." AI assistants don't see them, either. Noon's llms.txt says its pages return a markdown version on request. ## What Does Noon's 10x ROI Guarantee Require?

Under the guarantee, Noon promises a full refund if its platform doesn't save at least 10 times your annual subscription in recruiter time, valued at $60 an hour and measured over 12 months. Eligibility requires three things, per Noon's guarantee terms: a signed annual subscription, an account in good standing, and "Minimum Active Use" each week, with two non-qualifying months allowed. The Guarantee Target tops out at $1,000,000, and one-time implementation fees, professional services, usage overages, third-party fees, and separately priced add-ons aren't refundable.

Each week, your team must clear four minimums:

- At least one user logs in to Noon on three separate days each week.
- Your team creates at least one new role or search each week.
- Required integrations stay "connected and operational."
- You "complete any customer actions reasonably required" for Noon to keep working.

Teams get two non-qualifying months per year. Hit a third, and the guarantee ends; progress under a terminated guarantee "does not carry forward." Refunds arrive only after the full 12-month period, "once the measurement has been completed and confirmed."

The rules also changed within weeks. In a Wayback Machine capture of the pricing page from August 23, 2026, Noon called the guarantee opt-in and said it "credits the gap in free usage" if it fell short. Five weeks later, the same page promises to refund annual subscriptions "in full." A guarantee that changed shape once can change again, so get the version your rep quotes written into the contract.

### Is $60 an Hour a Fair Rate?

It's generous to the vendor. The median U.S. human resources specialist, the Bureau of Labor Statistics category that includes recruiters, earned $75,940 a year, or $36.51 an hour, in May 2025 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/human-resources-specialists.htm)). Noon does not publish a SOC 2 report or trust center; it shares security documents only under NDA.
