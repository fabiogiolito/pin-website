## What Is Juicebox in 2026?

Juicebox is an AI sourcing platform whose PeopleGPT engine searches 800M+ profiles from 30+ public sources, such as GitHub, Stack Overflow, and Google Scholar, in plain English. Type "backend engineer in Austin who has shipped Rust in production" and it returns a ranked shortlist. Founders David Paffenholz and Ishan Gupta came through Y Combinator's Summer 2022 batch.

Sequoia led a $30M Series A in 2025, when the company reported more than $10M in ARR and 2,500+ customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/juicebox-raises-30m-from-sequoia-to-revolutionize-hiring-with-llm-powered-search/), 2025). In March 2026, DST Global led an $80M Series B at an $850M valuation ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/juicebox-80-million-at-850-million-valuation-raised-for-ai-recruiting-platform/), 2026).

Agents first shipped in February 2025, and a rebuilt version relaunched in May 2026. Each one works a single open role around the clock, while recruiters review outreach before it sends and approve replies after a candidate engages. By then Juicebox had raised $116M and counted 5,000+ customers, according to [SiliconANGLE's coverage of the agent relaunch](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/20/exclusive-juicebox-autonomous-recruiting-agents-help-source-candidates-proactively/) (2026). Agent 4.0 and a Slack app arrived in August 2026, and every agent costs $199 a month on top of a seat. Pausing doesn't stop the meter: agents that are "sourcing, paused, or awaiting action each take up an Agent seat," per Juicebox's Agents help doc (checked September 2026).

Outreach pairs automated email with LinkedIn and call tasks, with no SMS step. ATS and CRM sync sits on the custom-priced Business plan, and the platform has no built-in interview scheduler. Five seats is the Growth ceiling, down from 10 until mid-2025, so a sixth recruiter forces that Business upgrade.

Good for: solo recruiters and small tech-hiring teams that want fast natural-language search and don't mind handling scheduling and ATS sync elsewhere. To see how that search engine holds up against human curation, read [Juicebox against Fetcher's curated model](/blog/fetcher-vs-juicebox/).

## What Is Metaview Sourcing, and When Did It Launch?

Metaview is an AI recruiting platform that started in London in 2018 as an interview notetaker and began sourcing in fall 2025, when it added an AI Sourcing agent. Founders Siadhal Magos and Shahriar Tajbakhsh have raised $50M in total, including a $35M Series B led by GV in June 2025 ([UNLEASH](https://www.unleash.ai/hr-technology/news/google-ventures-leads-35-million-series-b-funding-in-metaview), 2025).

Four sourcing-era releases shipped in under 12 months:

- **Fall 2025:** an AI Sourcing agent joins the platform.
- **February 2026:** native outreach sequences launch, starting with email.
- **June 2026:** fillmore, a Slack-only agent that researches candidates, sends outreach over email, LinkedIn, and WhatsApp, follows up, and books screening calls. Metaview's fillmore page says customers "only pay when you hire" but doesn't publish the fee.
- **August 2026:** Metaview acquires Reval, an AI-native recruiting firm, to speed up fillmore. At the time, fillmore was still waitlist-only, with 2,000+ teams in the queue ([HR Tech Feed](https://hrtechfeed.com/metaview-acquires-reval-to-accelerate-the-shift-from-ai-assistants-to-ai-coworkers/), 2026). By late September its page offered demos, but still no price.

Instead of a search box, Metaview sourcing starts from a role description. The agent returns matches, and only completed "agentic matches" count against your plan. Its profiles are licensed from third-party data providers, contact enrichment runs through FullEnrich (a listed subprocessor), and Metaview publishes no database size. Its own sourcing guide admits the data "is not exhaustive, and coverage can vary across locations, industries, and highly specialised roles." According to Metaview's Sourcing help article (checked September 2026), some candidates "may occasionally be unavailable, outdated, duplicated, incorrectly matched, or not suitable for the role."

Good for: in-house TA teams already running Metaview's notetaker that want sourcing from the same vendor, though depth is hard to judge without a published database size. A closer look at that gap sits in [how Metaview's sourcing stacks up against Pin](/blog/pin-vs-metaview/).

## Should a Recruiter or an Agent Drive the Search?

The biggest difference between Juicebox and Metaview in 2026 is recruiter control. Agents on Juicebox email only through sequences a recruiter has reviewed, and replies need human approval. Over in Slack, Metaview's fillmore picks its own channels across email, LinkedIn, and WhatsApp and sends on its own, but only inside Slack and at a per-hire fee Metaview doesn't publish. Pin sits across both camps: recruiters steer natural-language search with AI filters, while AI agents on every plan keep sourcing around the clock.

Skepticism about the "autonomous" label is warranted. Gartner estimates that only about 130 of the thousands of vendors selling agentic AI are real, and it predicts more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027 ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/over-40-agentic-ai-projects-100510349.html), 2025). Adoption runs broad but shallow, too: 69% of companies use AI somewhere in talent acquisition, yet just 18% use it broadly ([Aptitude Research and iCIMS](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-icims-and-aptitude-research-report-finds-candidates-are-outpacing-employers-in-ai-adoption-as-organizations-race-to-modernize-hiring-302758605.html), 2026).

**Here's what stood out to us** when we held these agent models up against Pin's [benchmark of 4,000,000+ recruiting messages](/blog/recruiting-outreach-benchmark-report/). A personal LinkedIn note out-replied automated email 3.4x, and pairing email with a LinkedIn touch lifted replies 2-4x over email alone at the same touch count. In the standard sequences of all three tools, email runs itself while the LinkedIn touch behind most of that lift remains a recruiter task. So the useful question isn't which agent is most autonomous. It's which workflow makes the high-reply human touch cheapest to do well: a shortlist you trust, a drafted message you can edit in seconds, and a task that lands at the right step. Three touches capture 93.2% of all replies in that same dataset, so a tool that nails the first three steps beats one running a ten-step sequence on autopilot. For agency recruiters whose name goes on every message, Pin is the best choice for that kind of controlled, high-reply outreach.

## Which Tool Has Better Candidate Data?

Trusting either tool's data blind is a mistake. Publicly, Juicebox lists a footprint of 800M+ profiles across 30+ public sources, yet G2 reviewers flag broken GitHub links and mismatched experience, and its own help center asks users to report fake profiles (see the complaints section below). No database size is published for Metaview at all, and its profiles are licensed from third-party data providers. Every completed agentic match counts toward the 200-profile monthly cap on Pro, even when a match turns out wrong. Treat head-to-head precision claims with care as well: Metaview's February 2026 comparison against Juicebox is a vendor-run score, not an independent result. Pin's multi-source database, by contrast, covers 850M+ profiles with 100% coverage in North America and Europe.

By 2028, one in four candidate profiles worldwide will be fake, Gartner predicts ([Gartner via PR Wire](https://prwire.com.au/pr/123287/gartner-survey-shows-only-26-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them), 2025). Recruiters already feel the squeeze: 66% say quality talent is getting harder to find, and applicants per open role have doubled since spring 2022. That same LinkedIn research, reported by [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-increasing-their-ai-usage-as-pressure-to-hire-intensifies/809051/) (2026), found 93% plan to grow their AI use this year.

So test it yourself. In any Juicebox vs Metaview trial, run three open reqs through each tool in the same week: one easy, one niche, one senior. Count the profiles you'd actually contact and how many have working contact data.

## What Do Users Complain About With Juicebox and Metaview?

Public complaints about Juicebox leave a dated trail. [Metaview's sourcing product](/blog/metaview-reviews/) has no independent track record at all. It launched in fall 2025, while Metaview's G2 profile already held 130 reviews by June 2025 ([G2 via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20250604013459/https://www.g2.com/products/metaview/reviews), 2025) and showed just 132 by September 2026. Effectively, the rating describes the notetaker, so buying Metaview sourcing means buying on the demo alone. Pricing history and help docs expose its sharpest limits.

On Juicebox, the complaints are specific and dated:

- **How the reviews were collected.** As recently as July 2026, Juicebox's G2 page held 12 reviews, and 11 were incentivized seller invites ([G2 via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20260713080606/https://www.g2.com/products/juicebox-peoplegpt/reviews), [page 2](https://web.archive.org/web/20260713080605/https://www.g2.com/products/juicebox-peoplegpt/reviews?page=2), 2026). It lists 62 today. The first page of [Pin's G2 reviews](https://web.archive.org/web/20260917102611/https://www.g2.com/products/pin/reviews) shows no incentivized reviews at all.
- **Mismatched experience.** One reviewer on that archived G2 page searched for five to seven years of experience and got profiles with "one to two years of experience, with internships or irrelevant experiences included."
- **Weaker results outside tech.** Another wrote that PeopleGPT "doesn't work as well for nontechnical roles," such as a marketing analyst.
- **Stale records.** A third flagged broken GitHub profile links: "I'd really like to see a more up-to-date database." Juicebox's own help center asks teams to report profiles that look "stale, inaccurate, or fake," including ones where "the URL may belong to a different person" (checked September 2026).
- **Demo vs contract.** A two-star Product Hunt reviewer who prepaid a year of Business found that ATS integration and hiring-manager seats shown in the demo were missing: "I was shocked to find these features were not included." Their verdict: "The sales demo does not accurately reflect what you get" ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/peoplegpt-by-juicebox/reviews?review=242696), August 2025).
- **"Fair" marks from hands-on testers.** [SourcrLab's August 2026 review](https://sourcrlab.com/tools/juicebox-peoplegpt) rated Juicebox only "Fair" on price transparency, value for money, and scalability, and put it bluntly: "Unlimited search is not unlimited contact."
- **Five-business-day support.** Its pricing page promises 24-hour replies on Growth and Business only, so Starter and Free get no guaranteed response time. The contract is looser still: Juicebox's Services Agreement (updated September 13, 2026) commits only to "commercially reasonable efforts to respond to all Helpdesk tickets within five (5) business days," by email, 9 to 5 Eastern.

Today it costs $179 for up to 5 seats, an 81% increase with half the seat cap. On Metaview, the limits are mostly structural:

- **The $100 plan got capped.** As late as March 6, 2026, Metaview's $100 sourcing plan advertised "Unlimited searches on paid plan" ([Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20260306085933/https://www.metaview.ai/pricing)). By March 11, that same $100 Pro plan was capped at 200 profiles a month, and unlimited profiles moved to a new $300 Max tier ([Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20260311112555/https://www.metaview.ai/pricing)). Keeping what the $100 plan used to include now costs three times as much.

## Outreach, AI Agents, and Interview Scheduling

Both tools send email automatically, and neither ships a general interview scheduler. LinkedIn and call steps become recruiter tasks in Juicebox. Metaview also sends WhatsApp messages automatically, while LinkedIn, phone, and SMS run as manual steps. Only fillmore breaks the pattern, choosing among email, LinkedIn, and WhatsApp itself.

Pin's multi-channel sequences pair automated email with AI-drafted LinkedIn and SMS tasks the recruiter sends, delivering 5x better response rates than industry averages.

Agent pricing splits three ways:

1. **Juicebox:** $199 per agent per month, with auto-shortlist, auto-email through reviewed sequences, or manual review modes.
2. **Metaview:** the sourcing agent sits inside the seat price, metered by profiles, while fillmore charges per hire.
3. **Pin:** AI agents on every plan, from one on Free to two per user on Professional and unlimited on Business.

Scheduling on Juicebox means pasting your own booking link into a sequence. Once a Calendly event is booked, Metaview's sequences stop, and fillmore books screening calls on its own. Pin includes automated interview scheduling with calendar sync, which closes the gap between a candidate's reply and a booked interview. That handoff matters when the median nonexecutive role still takes 39 days to fill ([SHRM benchmarking](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/recruiting-benchmarking/full-data-brief), 2026).

## Integrations, Security, and AI Hiring Compliance

Juicebox's homepage promises "41 ATS systems and 21 CRMs," yet its own help docs list three supported CRMs (Salesforce, HubSpot, and Affinity), call CRM sync "more constrained," and put every ATS and CRM connection on the Business plan. Pin sells ATS integration as an add-on from Solo up, while Juicebox won't sell it below a custom-priced Business contract. Metaview lists 47 ATS and CRM integrations plus calendar, Slack, and Zapier connections. Both hold SOC 2 Type 2, though Juicebox releases its SOC 2 report only to Business-plan customers. Beyond SOC 2, Metaview's trust center lists GDPR and CCPA, with customer data hosted on AWS in the UK. As for Pin, its SOC 2 Type 2 status, controls, and subprocessor list are public at trust.pin.com, with the report available on request. Alongside that, Pin supports 120+ ATS integrations and never feeds names, gender, or protected characteristics to its AI.

Regulation keeps shifting under all three:

- **New York City:** Local Law 144 has required annual bias audits and candidate notice for automated hiring tools since July 5, 2023 ([NYC DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/automated-employment-decision-tools.page)).
- **Illinois:** HB 3773 took effect January 1, 2026, adding an AI notice duty and a ban on discriminatory AI use ([Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/illinois-postpones-proposed-regulations-on-ai-in-employment/), 2026).
- **Colorado:** SB 26-189 replaced the original AI Act in May 2026 and takes effect January 1, 2027 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/colorado-governor-signs-sb-189), 2026).
- **European Union:** the AI Omnibus moved high-risk employment obligations from August 2, 2026 to December 2, 2027 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force), 2026).

In states with AI laws, 57% of HR professionals didn't know those regulations existed ([SHRM State of AI in HR](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/ai-hr-2026-from-hype-to-measured-human-centered-impact), 2026). Candidates are wary as well, since only 26% trust AI to evaluate them fairly (Gartner, 2025).

## Frequently Asked Questions
