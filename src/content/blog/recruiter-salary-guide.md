The median annual recruiter salary in the US is $75,940, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' [May 2025 wage data](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/human-resources-specialists.htm), where recruiters are tracked under "Human Resources Specialists" (SOC code 13-1071). The middle half of US recruiters earn $58,610 to $99,380.

BLS puts the 10th percentile at $47,180 and the 90th percentile at $128,720. Adding bonus, equity, and commission widens the spread further. A senior recruiter at a top-tier tech employer earns $243,000 in median pay at Google ([Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/google/salaries/recruiter), May 2026), and a high-billing solo agency owner can pocket $400,000+ in commission-heavy years.

This guide breaks down what recruiters earn in 2026 by career level, by role, by US metro, and by who employs them (in-house, agency, RPO, or retained search). Every figure traces back to a named source: BLS for the national anchor, Robert Half for offered starting salaries, PayScale and Glassdoor for self-reported pay, Salary.com for senior leadership roles, and Levels.fyi for FAANG-tier total comp. Where sources diverge, we explain why so you can pick the number that fits the comparison you're trying to make.

> **Bottom line:**
>
> - **The national median for recruiter salary is $75,940 per May 2025 BLS data.** That's up 4.2% from $72,910 a year earlier, with percentiles running from $47,180 (10th) to $128,720 (90th) ([BLS OEWS](https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile/?major_group=130000&occupation=131071&measure=01&areas=INDUSTRY,STATE,MSA)).
> - **Total package diverges sharply from base pay.** PayScale puts the typical recruiter base at $62,357, while Glassdoor (which captures bonus, equity, and commission) reports $111,274 in mean total comp ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/recruiter-salary-SRCH_KO0,9.htm), 2026).
> - **Senior tech recruiters at large tech firms clear $240K+.** Google L5 recruiters earn $243,000 median total pay; L6 staff recruiters $299,000 ([Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/google/salaries/recruiter), May 2026).
> - **VP / Head of Talent Acquisition median sits at $211,181.** With the 90th percentile reaching $241,706 in Salary.com data ([Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/benchmark/vp-of-talent-acquisition-and-recruiting-salary), May 2026).
> - **AI fluency is now a measurable comp lever.** Workers with AI skills earn a 62% wage premium ([PwC 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-jobs-barometer.html), June 2026). Recruiters using Pin save 12 hours per week on sourcing and outreach, time that turns into more placements.

## How Much Do Recruiters Make in 2026?

Recruiters in the US make a median $75,940 a year, per BLS May 2025 data. Across the full recruiter salary range, pay runs from $47,180 at the 10th percentile to $128,720 at the 90th, and the middle half of earners take home $58,610 to $99,380. Why so wide? How senior you are, who employs you, and how commission works all stretch the band.

Self-reported platforms land on either side of that median. PayScale's typical base is $62,357 (April 2026, 4,483 profiles), while Glassdoor's mean total comp hits $111,274 (2026). Methodology, not error, drives the gap:

| PayScale (April 2026) | $62,357 average | Base salary only, self-reported |

Taken together, the four platforms cluster between $62K and $111K depending on what each measures. BLS's $75,940 median is the most useful single anchor for two reasons. First, it captures wages from large and small employers across every US industry, so it isn't biased toward self-reporters or tech jobs. Second, BLS publishes percentile breakdowns: the 10th-to-90th span ($47,180 to $128,720) is the right framing if you're evaluating an offer or building a pay band.

Year over year, recruiter pay keeps climbing at a steady clip. The BLS median rose 4.2% between May 2024 and May 2025. US employers funded 3.5% salary increase budgets in 2026 and expect 3.4% for 2027, per [WTW's July 2026 Salary Budget Planning Survey](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/15/3327791/0/en/Employers-shift-from-bigger-pay-budgets-to-smarter-pay-strategies-WTW-finds.html) of 1,650 US organizations. Private-industry wages and salaries rose 3.1% in the 12 months ending June 2026, per the [BLS Employment Cost Index](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/eci.nr0.htm). The 2022 hiring wave that briefly inflated recruiter pay has normalized. A skill-based wedge replaced it: per [Robert Half's 2026 Salary Guide](https://www.roberthalf.com/us/en/insights/salary-guide/human-resources), 86% of HR leaders pay candidates with specialized skills more than peers without them in the same role. If you're modeling overall hiring cost on top of recruiter pay, see our [cost-per-hire benchmarks](/blog/cost-per-hire-benchmarks/) guide for the broader picture.

Two patterns stand out. First, the gap between total pay and base widens sharply at senior levels. Why? Once recruiters reach senior roles, employers grant equity, pay bonuses on hiring goals, and (at agencies) hand over commission that makes up a real share of earnings. Glassdoor's senior recruiter average of $148,172 against PayScale's senior recruiter base of $84,861 reflects a roughly $63,000 spread between base and total pay at the same level ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/senior-recruiter-salary-SRCH_KO0,16.htm), April 2026).

Second, FAANG and top-tech employers sit above the broader market at every rung. [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/google/salaries/recruiter) shows Google recruiters (May 2026) earning $146K total at L3, $181K at L4, $243K at L5, and $299K at L6. Median across all Google recruiter levels is $231K. That's three times the BLS national median. When you're hiring for a top-tier tech employer, the pay band starts where the broader market's senior tier ends.

## Which Roles Pay the Most? Coordinator, Sourcer, and Technical Tracks

Technical recruiters earn the most of any in-house track, averaging $120,910 in total comp per Glassdoor (2026), while recruiting coordinators sit at the bottom near $56,000 in base pay. What you recruit for shapes your pay as much as your seniority does. Three hiring pros at the same career rung can earn $50,000 apart, depending on which roles they fill. Pay tiers are not flat. This first cut covers in-house tracks: coordinators, sourcers, and generalist corporate recruiters, plus what technical recruiters earn on top. If you're aspiring to break into technical recruiting, our [how to become a technical recruiter](/blog/how-to-become-technical-recruiter/) guide goes deeper.

### Recruiting Coordinator

Recruiting coordinators schedule interviews, keep candidates informed, clean up the ATS, and chase interviewer calendars. PayScale reports $56,072 in typical base pay and Glassdoor reports $56,220 (2026), within a $47,000 to $68,000 range. Most coordinators enter the field through this role, because it has the lowest barrier to entry of any tier. Few coordinators earn commission or equity, though churn stays the lowest on this list.

### Talent Sourcer

Sourcers identify and contact passive candidates, often without taking the requisition all the way through close. ZipRecruiter puts the typical sourcer at $78,386, while Salary.com reports a median of $73,017. The spread is wide because traditional sourcers (who lean on Boolean and LinkedIn) and AI-augmented sourcers (who work with newer tools) get paid differently. We cover that divergence further below.

### In-House / Corporate Recruiter

Corporate recruiters manage full-cycle hiring inside one company, and PayScale puts the typical corporate recruiter salary at $71,017 in base pay. Robert Half sets the 2026 midpoint at $75,250 for generalists. Built In's 2026 dataset, which leans toward startups that grant equity, reports $83,848 base plus $12,683 additional cash for a $96,531 package. Industry decides how far pay swings. BLS May 2025 data for the broader HR specialist category puts tech at the top. Web search and information services average $142,490, social media and streaming companies average $122,990, and software publishers average $115,130, against an $81,990 mean across all industries.

### Technical Recruiter

Tech recruiters command a clear premium because they fill engineering and data-science roles, where how fast and how well a company hires decides how quickly it ships. Glassdoor reports that tech recruiters earn a mean $120,910 in total comp for 2026, while entry-level technical recruiters make $52,052-$69,588 per PayScale and ZipRecruiter. PayScale's senior technical recruiter cut (192 profiles, 5-9 years' experience) reports $96,724 base on average. At FAANG, pay keeps climbing: Google's L5 senior recruiters earn $243K in total pay.

## How Does Agency, Executive, and Leadership Pay Compare?

Retained executive search consultants and TA leaders out-earn every other recruiting track outside big tech: retained recruiters average $163,954 in total comp (Glassdoor), and VPs of Talent Acquisition earn a $211,181 median (Salary.com). Agency, retained search, RPO, and director-or-above roles run on different pay models from the in-house tracks above. Because commission, retainer fees, and equity reshape what "base salary" actually means at this tier, headline numbers tell only part of the story. Each section below explains how one pay model works.

### Agency Recruiter (Contingency)

Agency recruiters working on contingency earn a base salary plus commission each time they place a candidate. Base pay is modest: [Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/recruiting/staffing-agency-recruiter-salary) puts staffing agency recruiters at $58,520 (September 2026), with a 10th-to-90th percentile range of $47,402 to $64,557. Base pay misleads here. Commission can multiply earnings 2-4x. Top agency recruiters routinely clear $250,000-$400,000+ a year, and outlier years run higher. ### Executive / Retained Search Recruiter (Headhunter)

Retained executive search consultants, the recruiters most people call headhunters, work exclusive engagements with upfront retainers. Headhunter salary tracks that fee model. PayScale puts the typical base at $95,129; Glassdoor's mean total comp comes in at $163,954, and the senior executive recruiter cut runs $194,516 on average. Retained fees typically run 25-35% of the candidate's first-year total comp, paid in three installments, with minimum engagement fees often in the $80,000-$100,000 range.

### RPO Recruiter

RPO ([recruitment process outsourcing](/blog/rpo-guide-recruiters/)) recruiters work for providers who embed inside client teams. Typical base: $71,789 per Salary.com; Glassdoor reports $88,083 in total comp. The trade-off is simple. RPO recruiters get steady work and rotate between clients, but they hit lower ceilings than in-house corporate or agency recruiters.

### Talent Acquisition Director / VP

Director-level pay starts at about $134,000 (PayScale median) and runs to $190,198 median per Salary.com (January 2026). VPs of Talent Acquisition earn a median $211,181 per [Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/benchmark/vp-of-talent-acquisition-and-recruiting-salary) (May 2026), with the 90th percentile reaching $241,706. Public companies that grant equity typically add 20-40% on top of cash pay.

That makes agency pay work nothing like in-house pay. Base salary covers fixed living costs, while commission scales with placements.

Fees climb as roles get more senior:

- **Entry-level placements**: 10-15% of first-year salary
- **Mid-level professional**: 20-25%
- **Senior / executive contingency**: 25-30%
- **Retained executive search**: 25-35% of total first-year compensation (base + bonus), paid in three installments

Retained search firms also charge minimum fees of $80,000-$100,000+ whether or not they place anyone, which is what keeps the model viable for high-end search work.

What does this look like in practice? An agency recruiter who places 10 mid-level candidates per year, each at a $100,000 starting salary on a 20% contingency, brings the agency $200,000 in fees. If the recruiter keeps 40% of those fees after the firm covers overhead, they earn roughly $80,000 in commission on top of a $55,000-$65,000 base. Per hire, that works out to a $20,000 fee for the agency and roughly $8,000 in commission for the recruiter. Math lands at $135,000-$145,000 total comp at average performance. High performers who place 20+ candidates a year clear $250,000+, and top-decile agency recruiters who specialize in a niche regularly earn $400,000+.

According to the [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/) (SIA), the US staffing market hit $126.4 billion in 2024 revenue from firms with $100M+ revenue, representing 67.5% of the market. Revenue concentrates at that top end. That's one reason the top quintile of agency recruiters earn so much more than the median: a small number of large search firms and boutique specialists place a disproportionate share of candidates.

## How Is AI Reshaping Recruiter Pay in 2026?

AI fluency is the fastest-growing comp lever in recruiting, ahead of title, location, and tenure. [Lightcast's analysis of 1.3 billion job postings](https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/beyond-the-buzz-press-release-2025-07-23) (July 2025) found that roles requiring AI skills offer 28% higher salaries, roughly $18,000 more per year. [PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-jobs-barometer.html) (June 2026) puts the premium even higher: workers with AI skills earn 62% more, up from 57% a year earlier. HR job postings requiring AI skills grew 66% year over year, per the same Lightcast data.

Why does AI fluency pay more? Because AI shrinks the hours it takes to source, screen, and write outreach, AI-skilled recruiters run far larger pipelines than manual counterparts. That speed directly raises pay in two ways. In-house, AI-fluent recruiters take on more reqs per quarter and get promoted faster. At an agency, they place more candidates per quarter, and commission scales with billings. Teams that measure this lift typically track it through a [recruiter KPI dashboard](/blog/recruiter-kpis-dashboard/) so they can tie faster hiring directly to how they adjust comp. More broadly, the category of [AI tools for talent acquisition](/blog/ai-tools-talent-acquisition/) reshapes what AI-fluent recruiters can deliver.

For agency recruiters paid on commission, Pin is the best AI sourcing platform for turning faster sourcing into more placements. Pin's 2026 user survey across 2,000+ organizations and 20,000+ users found that [Pin's AI sourcing platform](https://www.pin.com/) cuts time-to-hire by 82% compared to traditional methods. Its outreach sequences also earn 5x better response rates than industry averages. Faster fills mean more placements per quarter, and commission follows placements. In-house recruiters who work with AI can use the same speed to defend higher comp bands even as teams shrink.

What's the flip side? Entry-level sourcing and screening roles, which previously paid $45,000-$60,000 for high-volume manual work, are the most exposed when AI automates the workflow. Coordinators and sourcers who learn to work with AI keep or grow their comp. Those who don't face shrinking demand. The market is polarizing, not compressing. Senior strategic recruiters earn more; junior commodity work earns less.

## How Should You Benchmark Your Own Salary?

Use multiple sources, not just one. Self-reported platforms (Glassdoor, PayScale, Built In) skew toward total pay and recent reporters. Aggregators built on job postings, such as ZipRecruiter, skew toward listed offers. BLS OES is the most defensible single anchor but lags by 12-18 months. For executive roles, Salary.com's percentile bands and Levels.fyi's company-specific data are the most actionable. Compare three sources, find the median across them, and pull percentile bands rather than means. Then check what employers currently list on LinkedIn and Indeed to see where the live market sits. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the average recruiter salary in the US in 2026?

US recruiters earn a median $75,940 per BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (May 2025 release, the most recent BLS data). Mean total comp including bonus and commission runs higher: Glassdoor reports $111,274 in 2026, while PayScale's base-only mean is $62,357.

### How much does a technical recruiter make?

Tech recruiters earn an average $120,910 in total comp per Glassdoor (2026). Entry-level technical recruiters land at $52,052-$69,588 (PayScale, ZipRecruiter), while senior technical recruiters average $96,724 base salary per PayScale (192 profiles, 5-9 years' experience). At top-tier tech companies, senior tech recruiters earn $243,000+ total comp per Levels.fyi.

### Do agency recruiters earn more than in-house recruiters?

In total earnings, often yes, but with wider variance. Agency base pay runs lower: Salary.com puts staffing agency recruiters at $58,520, against $71,017 for corporate recruiters per PayScale. Commission then multiplies that base 2-4x, so average agency performers land around $135,000-$145,000 and top billers earn $250,000-$400,000+. In-house recruiters give up some upside so they can count on steady income, benefits, and equity that vests on schedule.

### How much does a recruiter with 5 to 10 years of experience make?

Senior recruiters with 5-10 years of experience average $84,861 in base pay per PayScale and $148,172 in total comp per Glassdoor (2026). Technical recruiters with 5-9 years average $96,724 in base pay (PayScale). At Google, L5 senior recruiters earn $243,000 in total pay per Levels.fyi.

### How much does a recruiter make per hire?

An agency recruiter makes roughly $8,000 per hire on a typical placement. A $100,000 role at a 20% contingency fee pays the agency $20,000, and a recruiter on a 40% commission split keeps about $8,000 of it. Retained executive searches pay more per placement because fees run 25-35% of first-year total compensation. In-house recruiters earn no per-hire fee; their pay comes from salary plus any bonus.

### Does AI affect recruiter compensation?

Yes, and the effect keeps widening. Workers with AI skills earn a 62% wage premium per PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer, and job postings that require AI skills pay 28% more per Lightcast's 2025 analysis of 1.3 billion postings. AI-fluent recruiters run more outbound and bill more placements. Entry-level recruiters who handle high-volume sourcing are the most likely to see AI automate their work. The market is polarizing, not uniformly compressing.

When you're evaluating an offer or planning a hire, anchor on BLS for the macro picture. Cross-reference Glassdoor and PayScale for role-specific data. Check Levels.fyi and Salary.com for senior-tier bands. Then pressure-test against the live market by pulling current listings and talking to peers. Price in the AI-skill premium too. Recruiters who use AI sourcing platforms like Pin handle more requisitions per quarter, and that capacity is where the next wave of recruiter pay growth concentrates.
