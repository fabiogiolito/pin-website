The seekout vs linkedin recruiter decision is a split verdict. SeekOut is the best choice for technical and security-cleared sourcing, while LinkedIn Recruiter is the best choice for non-technical hiring and real-time candidate signals. At contract level both cost 5-10x more than [Pin](https://www.pin.com/), without matching Pin's 5x better outreach response rates. [SeekOut's median annual contract](/blog/seekout-pricing/) is $20,000 per [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/seekout) (based on 65 purchases). Full LinkedIn Recruiter seats run $8,000-$12,000 per seat per year, per Vendr's LinkedIn marketplace data from February 2026. Pin, by contrast, starts at $100/mo with a free tier, indexes 850M+ multi-source profiles, and automates sourcing, outreach, and scheduling in one workflow.

Sourcing-tool trends shifted fast in 2025. Generative AI adoption among recruiting teams rose from 27% to 37% year over year, per LinkedIn's 2025 Future of Recruiting report, and the pace has not let up: 93% of talent acquisition professionals plan to expand their AI use in 2026, per [HR Dive's January 2026 reporting](https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-increasing-their-ai-usage-as-pressure-to-hire-intensifies/809051/). Gartner named AI agents the number-one talent acquisition trend for 2026 in its [October 2025 press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-07-gartner-says-ai-revolution-and-cost-pressures-are-two-forces-driving-the-top-four-trends-for-talent-acquisition-in-2026). Both platforms rolled out AI agents to keep up (SeekOut Spot and LinkedIn Hiring Assistant). Readers still need a comparison across data breadth, pricing, outreach, and which tool fits which recruiter. This guide delivers that.

What follows is a decision breakdown across six categories: pricing, database coverage, outreach capabilities, AI features, search and integrations, and use-case fit. Every data point is sourced. Then we explain where Pin is the better answer to the underlying problem both tools only partially solve.

## Key Takeaways

- **Price gap is 5-10x vs Pin at the contract level.** SeekOut's median annual contract is $20K and full LinkedIn Recruiter seats run $8,000-$12,000 each ([Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/seekout), February 2026). Pin starts at $100/mo with a free tier.
- **SeekOut still wins on cleared and deep-technical talent, but the gap narrowed.** Patents, publications, and a 12-level clearance filter covering 3.7M+ cleared candidates surface people LinkedIn misses. GitHub alone is no longer SeekOut's moat, because LinkedIn now enriches Hiring Assistant with external GitHub signals.
- **LinkedIn Recruiter wins on network size and real-time data.** 1.3B registered members (per LinkedIn's Pressroom, January 2026), active self-maintained profiles, and the Hiring Assistant AI agent (launched globally September 2025) make it strong for high-volume, non-technical hiring.
- **Both cap outreach volume.** Recruiter Lite gives 30 InMails per month, full Recruiter gives 150, and Recruiter Professional Services gives 100. SeekOut sends email only, with no native InMail and no SMS. Pin's multi-channel sequences (email, LinkedIn, and SMS) carry no seat-based cap and deliver 5x better response rates than industry averages.
- **SeekOut changed CEOs in 2026.** Sean Thompson took over as chief executive effective May 4, 2026, with co-founder Anoop Gupta moving to executive chairman. That followed a 30% workforce cut in May 2024 and a 7% reduction in October 2023, per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/20/seekout-layoffs-30percent-talent-search-tigerglobal/). Buyers signing a multi-year contract should weigh both.

## What Do SeekOut and LinkedIn Recruiter Actually Cost in 2026?

Real contract prices land far above the published entry tiers: SeekOut's median annual contract runs $20,000, and a full LinkedIn Recruiter seat runs $8,000-$12,000 a year. Vendors on both sides publish an entry price and keep the enterprise number behind a sales call. Here's what buyers actually pay, from procurement data and public sources.

### SeekOut pricing (2026)

SeekOut restructured its entry tier in 2026. The old Recruit Lite plan ($2,150/year, single recruiter) is gone, replaced by a self-serve tier. SeekOut's own pricing page now lists four tiers, and only the entry tier carries a public number:

- **Recruit Core**: $149/month billed annually ($1,788/year) or $179/month billed monthly, and it includes 3 seats (500 monthly contact credits, 1,000 exports). A 14-day free trial runs with no credit card and no contract.
- **Recruit Sourcing**: custom per-seat pricing (750 contacts/mo per seat, 5,000 exports per seat)
- **Recruit Sourcing + Integration**: custom pricing, adds ATS integration
- **Recruit Full Recruiting Funnel**: custom pricing, adds end-to-end AI tooling and enterprise support

That restructure matters more than it looks. At three seats for $1,788/year, Recruit Core works out to roughly $596 per seat, which undercuts LinkedIn Recruiter Lite's per-seat cost by a wide margin and reverses the old entry-price story.

To see what buyers actually pay above the self-serve tier, [Vendr's marketplace](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/seekout) aggregates 65 verified SeekOut purchases:

- **Median annual contract**: $20,000
- **Range**: $5,820 to $54,920
- **Per-seat bands**: Essentials $3,000-$6,000, Professional $5,000-$9,000, Enterprise $8,000-$15,000+ per seat per year
- **Average buyer saving**: 16% off the first quote

Vendr last updated that dataset in February 2026. Contract plans bill annually.

### LinkedIn Recruiter pricing (2026)

On the LinkedIn side, no price is publicly listed at all any more. During 2026 LinkedIn pulled the number from its own Recruiter Lite page and replaced it with a free-trial call to action. Every figure below therefore comes from procurement data or widespread reporting, not from the vendor:

- **Recruiter Lite**: widely reported at $170/month ($1,680/year) for a single seat, though LinkedIn no longer publishes it. Lite is capped at 20 search filters and third-degree connections only.
- **LinkedIn Recruiter (full platform)**: $8,000-$12,000 per seat per year, per Vendr's LinkedIn marketplace data from February 2026. Vendr puts the median LinkedIn contract at $38,480/year across 1,698 deals.
- **Recruiter Professional Services (RPS)**: agency-specific product, sales-quoted
- **Hiring Assistant**: sold as a paid add-on to LinkedIn Recruiter, not bundled
- **LinkedIn Talent Insights (analytics add-on)**: annual packages from £9,400 for one seat up to £82,850 for ten seats in LinkedIn's UK G-Cloud price list (2025)

Note that LinkedIn has also retired the "Recruiter Corporate" name; its current SKUs are Recruiter Lite, LinkedIn Recruiter, and Recruiter Professional Services. Auto-renewal is the default.

## Which Platform Has Better Candidate Coverage?

Coverage splits cleanly by role type: LinkedIn Recruiter's 1.3B registered members win for commercial hiring, while SeekOut's multi-source index wins for technical talent. Raw network size misleads on both sides. What matters is what's searchable, what's current, and what your competitors can't easily find.

### LinkedIn Recruiter's network

As of January 2026, LinkedIn has 1.3 billion registered members, per LinkedIn's Pressroom. Every minute, the platform takes in about 8,200 job applications. One catch complicates that number. Microsoft, LinkedIn's parent, stopped publishing exact monthly-active-user counts after crossing 1 billion, though third-party estimates put LinkedIn MAU at 310-350 million. Roughly 70-75% of registered members don't log in monthly, which means their profile data, job history, and "Open to Work" signals can be months or years stale.

Still, for non-technical roles like sales, marketing, operations, and general business, LinkedIn Recruiter's coverage is hard to match. Candidates maintain their own profiles, companies are verified, and activity signals (last active, job-seeking status) are live.

### SeekOut's aggregated database

On the SeekOut side, the homepage claims 1B+ candidate profiles, aggregated from:

- Public LinkedIn data
- GitHub (active repos, commit history, languages)
- Stack Overflow (reputation, tags)
- Patent filings (USPTO, WIPO)
- Academic publications (96M+ papers)
- Conference speaker lists and professional certifications
- ATS rediscovery data (candidates already in your pipeline who were rejected or ghosted)

That breadth is SeekOut's real structural advantage. Consider a software engineer with a sparse LinkedIn profile but an active GitHub and a handful of patents: effectively invisible inside LinkedIn Recruiter, but surfaced by SeekOut.

### Where the coverage gap actually shows up

Three specific scenarios flip the answer:

1. **Deep technical sourcing**: native Stack Overflow, patent, and publication indexes still make SeekOut the stronger tool for software, ML, and security researchers. LinkedIn closed part of this gap in 2026 by enriching Hiring Assistant with external GitHub signals, but it indexes none of the other three.
2. **Cleared talent**: SeekOut's 12-level security clearance filter (including TS/SCI and Full Scope Poly) is purpose-built for defense, aerospace, and federal contracting. No equivalent filter exists on LinkedIn.
3. **High-volume commercial hiring**: LinkedIn's live activity signals and broader member base make it stronger for sales, marketing, customer success, and general operations roles where GitHub activity doesn't apply.

Yet a blind spot survives on both sides. Neither reaches people who live primarily outside LinkedIn and escape SeekOut's crawlers. Pin's 850M+ profiles pull from multiple professional networks, technical communities, patent databases, and the broader web, with real-time deliverability across email, LinkedIn, and SMS outreach that neither SeekOut nor LinkedIn Recruiter ties together.

## How Do Outreach Capabilities Compare?

Outreach caps decide this one: LinkedIn Recruiter allows 30-150 InMails a month, and SeekOut sends email only, with no native InMail and no SMS. This is the category that separates a "sourcing tool" from a "full recruiting workflow."

### LinkedIn Recruiter outreach

On LinkedIn Recruiter, outreach is built around InMail. That's both its advantage and its constraint:

- **Deliverability**: InMails land in a candidate's LinkedIn inbox with recognizable branding, which lifts response rates above cold email's 1-5%. Worth knowing before you build a business case on a specific number: LinkedIn's own talent-blog analysis reports InMail performance only as lift against an undisclosed global average, never as an absolute response rate.
- **Credit limits**: 30 InMails per month on Recruiter Lite. 150 per month on Corporate. Additional credits cost ~$10 each (per LinkedIn's official InMail limit documentation). High-volume sourcers hit the ceiling fast.
- **No email, no SMS**: LinkedIn Recruiter doesn't send personal email or SMS. Candidates who don't check LinkedIn don't see the message.

For teams thinking about [LinkedIn InMail automation](/blog/linkedin-inmail-automation/) as a workaround, LinkedIn's own terms of service restrict third-party automation inside the platform, and accounts get restricted or banned for aggressive bulk sending.

### SeekOut outreach

On the SeekOut side, email campaigns run up to roughly 1,000 emails per campaign per day, with LinkedIn step reminders alongside:

- **Email campaigns**: SeekOut finds personal email and phone numbers and runs multi-step email sequences. Whether those emails land depends on how carefully you warm the domain and guard sender reputation.
- **LinkedIn steps**: SeekOut prompts the recruiter to log into LinkedIn and send messages one at a time.
- **No native InMail send**: SeekOut can't send InMails on your behalf.
- **No native SMS**: Text messaging isn't in the platform.

Volume is high on SeekOut's side. Bounces are common too, though, without careful domain setup. And neither platform integrates outreach with interview scheduling, so recruiters still bounce between tools to confirm times.

### The Pin gap

Structurally, Pin closes that gap. Multi-channel sequences across email, LinkedIn, and SMS run from a single workflow and deliver 5x better response rates than industry averages. Weigh that against cold email's 1-5% baseline. Seat credits never cap the volume. Those mechanics are what make Pin the [most-adopted LinkedIn Recruiter alternative](/blog/pin-vs-linkedin-recruiter/) for teams tired of running email, InMail, and SMS through three separate tools.

## How Do the AI Agents (Hiring Assistant vs Spot) Compare?

Execution differs sharply: Hiring Assistant is software embedded in LinkedIn's own data graph, while Spot wraps a human recruiting service around SeekOut's search. Each platform shipped its agent in the past 18 months.

### LinkedIn Hiring Assistant

Originally launched to charter customers in October 2024, Hiring Assistant became globally available in September 2025, per LinkedIn's own announcement. LinkedIn refreshed its published performance numbers in 2026, and all three moved:

- Saves 1.5 hours per role identifying top-qualified applicants (revised down from the 4+ hours LinkedIn quoted at launch)
- Users view 81% fewer profiles to find a qualified match (up from 62%)
- 66% higher InMail acceptance rates versus traditional sourcing (revised from 69%)

LinkedIn footnotes those figures to its own data from January 2026. Named customer results include Expedia Group cutting time-to-hire by 30 days, and NES Fircroft reporting a 65% InMail acceptance rate on Hiring-Assistant-sourced candidates against 39% from manual sourcing.

Functionally, Hiring Assistant reads a job description, builds a sourcing strategy, screens for the basics (whether a candidate can work legally, fits the location, and will relocate), ranks candidates by evidence, and integrates with ATS and Microsoft Teams. Early customers include AMD, Chewy, Expedia Group, Microsoft, Siemens, and Wipro. The English-only limitation is gone: Hiring Assistant now runs in English, French, German, Dutch, Spanish, Portuguese, and Italian. Budget for it separately, though, because LinkedIn sells it as a paid add-on to Recruiter rather than bundling it.

One advantage stands out: Hiring Assistant sits inside LinkedIn's data, so it sees real-time activity signals, last-login dates, and "Open to Work" status that external tools can't access. LinkedIn has also started pulling credible external signals, GitHub among them, into Hiring Assistant sourcing, which narrows one of SeekOut's long-standing technical advantages.

### SeekOut Spot

Launched in 2025, SeekOut Spot is a hybrid AI + human recruiter product pitched as a replacement for traditional agencies:

- Interview-ready candidates in under two weeks (per SeekOut's own Spot announcement)
- 70% lower cost than alternatives, plus claims of 5x more candidates evaluated and 50% faster time-to-fill (all SeekOut's own numbers)
- Per-role, per-contractor, or outcome-aligned pricing
- No long-term contract required

Spot also carries more strategic weight than it did a year ago. SeekOut's incoming CEO named scaling Spot, alongside acquiring recruiting agencies, as his primary focus.

Spot works well for teams that want candidates sourced and screened for them, though it's a services engagement more than a software workflow, a meaningful architectural difference from Hiring Assistant.

### How they actually compare

Hiring Assistant is software-native and embedded in LinkedIn's data graph. Spot wraps a service around SeekOut's search tools. Two different products, really. One sells you software; the other sells you outcomes, and that distinction decides which procurement process you end up in. For in-house teams wanting AI to reduce recruiter workload, Hiring Assistant is the tighter fit. If you need outsourced sourcing without signing a full agency contract, Spot is worth evaluating. Neither handles end-to-end multi-channel outreach and interview scheduling, which is where recruiters still patch in a third tool.

## What About Search, Filters, and ATS Integrations?

### Search filter depth

On LinkedIn Recruiter, 40+ advanced filters ship by default: title, skills, location, company size, diversity markers, education, spoken language, relocation willingness, "Open to Work," and company tenure. G2 scores LinkedIn Recruiter 9.2/10 on sourcing and candidate identification.

By contrast, SeekOut matches LinkedIn on core filters and adds technical ones: GitHub language, patent count, Stack Overflow reputation, publication count, security clearance (12 levels, AI-inferred), and granular diversity markers. Its Bias Reducer mode actively hides race and gender indicators during review, a differentiator for teams building structured screening.

G2's comparison shows SeekOut at 4.5/5 across 759 reviews with a 9.3/10 Customer Support score. Ratings for LinkedIn Recruiter land near 4.3-4.5/5 across roughly 400 reviews, with an 8.2/10 support score, though G2 publishes no clean public figure for it.
