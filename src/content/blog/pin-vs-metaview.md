## What's the Core Difference Between Pin and Metaview?

Pin was built for sourcing from day one; Metaview bolted a sourcing agent onto an interview notetaker. Think of Pin as a proactive engine. It scans 850M+ profiles, drafts personalized messages for email, LinkedIn, and SMS steps, and books interviews automatically. Where Pin finds people, Metaview's roots are in joining interviews and turning the conversations into structured data. Per the [Gem 2026 Recruiting Benchmarks Report](https://www.gem.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-2026-recruiting-benchmarks-report), sourced talent is nearly 8x more likely to be hired than inbound applicants. Such a gap explains why a pipeline-first platform creates different ROI than a tool focused on the interview layer.

Here is where Metaview's 2025-2026 product expansion matters. Founded in 2018 and built around an AI Notetaker (covered by [TechCrunch in March 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/28/metaviews-tool-records-interview-notes-so-that-hiring-managers-dont-have-to/)), the company has spent the last year stacking agents on top of that product. September 2025 is when its sourcing page first appears in the Internet Archive; outreach sequences followed in February 2026, and an autonomous outbound agent, fillmore, was announced in June 2026. When Metaview bought recruiting firm Reval in August 2026, fillmore was still waitlist-only ([Business Wire](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/metaview-acquires-reval-accelerate-shift-130000064.html), August 2026).

None of that closes the discovery gap. [Metaview's AI Sourcing agent](/blog/juicebox-vs-metaview/) never says how many profiles it searches, and its own sourcing guide admits the data "is not exhaustive, and coverage can vary across locations, industries, and highly specialised roles." Pin publishes its scale: 850M+ profiles with 100% coverage in North America and Europe, drawn from professional networks, GitHub, Stack Overflow, patents, and academic publications.

**Having built Pin** (and Interseller before it, which the team sold to Greenhouse), our view on this is shaped by a decade of watching recruiters lose to the same problem: the candidates worth hiring rarely apply. Fewer than 1% of the profiles in a sample drawn from Pin's 850M+ index broadcast any active job-seeking signal (Pin first-party data, 2026). Everyone else, 99%+ of the pool, is passive talent who never surfaces in an applicant queue, however strong the job post. Talent worth hiring has to be found and engaged before someone is thinking about a move. Among Pin customers, 91% reduced or eliminated LinkedIn Recruiter spend after switching (Pin 2026 user survey), because a deep multi-source database removes the inbound-only ceiling. For more on why teams are [moving off LinkedIn Recruiter](/blog/leaving-linkedin-recruiter/) specifically, the dynamics extend well beyond price. Roles fill in 14 days on average (Pin 2026 user survey), roughly 67% faster than the 42-to-44-day U.S. industry baseline reported by [HR.com's Future of Recruitment Technologies 2025-26](https://mitratech.com/resource-hub/blog/what-2025-time-to-fill-benchmarks-reveal-about-hiring-agility-and-risk/). That gap is not a measurement quirk. It is the difference between a tool that finds candidates and a tool that processes the ones who already showed up.

## Which Platform Has Better Candidate Sourcing?

Proactive candidate discovery is where Pin's architectural advantage is largest. With 850M+ profiles aggregated from professional networks, GitHub, Stack Overflow, open-source contributions, patents, and academic publications, Pin's index carries thousands of data points per profile. Across the same database, Boolean strings, natural language queries, and AI matching all work without switching surfaces. Whether you are filling a needle-in-a-haystack engineer role with cryptography patents or hiring 40 nurse practitioners across three regions, the workflow runs through one interface.

Talent search is a new line of business for Metaview, launched in fall 2025, and its entry plan is already capped. Today the $100 Pro seat stops at 200 sourced profiles a month. It's still sold as "Proactive, 24/7 candidate search," yet Metaview's help center reserves scheduled search reruns for Max and Enterprise. Separately, the help center warns that some sourced candidates "may occasionally be unavailable, outdated, duplicated, incorrectly matched, or not suitable for the role." According to the [Gem 2026 Recruiting Benchmarks Report](https://www.gem.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-2026-recruiting-benchmarks-report), direct sourcing now drives 11% of hires from just 2.6% of applications (a 4x efficiency rate), and 46% of sourced hires come from rediscovered candidates (up from 26% in 2021). Both numbers reward a deep, multi-source database recruiters can return to. A search whose own vendor calls its coverage "not exhaustive" has a lower ceiling.

Nick Poloni, President at Cascadia Search Group, described what proactive talent search with the largest multi-source candidate database in the industry actually delivered for his agency:

> "I jumped into Pin solo toward the end of 2025 and closed out the year with over $1M in billings during just the final 4 months, no team, no agency. The sourcing data is incredible, scanning 850M+ profiles with recruiter-level precision to uncover perfect-fit candidates I'd never find otherwise."

The same engine extends to any web page through Pin's Chrome extension. From a LinkedIn page, a GitHub commit log, or a company directory, you can pull a profile directly into a sequence.

## How Does Outreach and Engagement Compare?

Across email, LinkedIn, and SMS, Pin runs sequences in a single coordinated workflow. The reported 5x better response rates than industry averages come from the platform-wide Pin 2026 user survey, not a hand-picked case study. Per the [SHRM State of AI in HR 2026 Report](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/state-of-ai-hr-2026/full-report), AI use across HR tasks reached 43% in 2026, up from 26% in 2024. Adoption is uneven: some platforms have built the engagement layer that turns AI talent search into actual conversations, while others have not.

On Metaview's side, outreach sequences only launched in February 2026, tied to the AI Sourcing agent. No platform-wide response rate or channel breakdown has been disclosed, so there is no published number to set against Pin's 5x figure.

Metaview markets Sourcing as a "Truly autonomous AI sourcing agent." Its own guidance says Sourcing does not "Automatically contact, progress, or reject any candidate," and its Sourcing Copilot page says "The agent doesn't refine itself." Sequences also stop when a candidate books through Calendly, because Sourcing has no scheduling step of its own. Inside Pin, interview scheduling, calendar syncing, and confirmations run automatically in the same workflow.

## What Does Metaview's Interview Intelligence Cost?

Teams that only need recorded notes will find Metaview's interview agents good for that job, though Inaccuracy was the most-tagged con on its G2 page in a [June 2025 snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20250604013459/https://www.g2.com/products/metaview/reviews) (12 mentions). The same snapshot shows reviewers describing a bot that "mixes up languages," struggles with accented speakers, and sometimes fails to join interviews when its ATS link drops. All of its interview-side features (AI Notetaker, Automated Scorecards, AI Reports, AI Answers, Snippets, and TLDR summaries) are sold across agents that each bill on their own:

- **Notetaker Pro:** now listed only at $60 per user per month. March 2026's $50 annual option is gone.
- **Application Review Pro:** $150 a month for 500 reviews.
- **Screening:** the first 25 screens are free, with no published rate after that.
- **Reports:** "an add-on you can include in your subscription for a flat fee," with the fee undisclosed.

Those agents stack on top of the sourcing bill rather than replacing any of it. Pin doesn't record or transcribe interviews, and it doesn't need to for the job it does: if your problem is too few qualified candidates, a notetaker won't fix it. For most teams in 2026, candidate-flow volume is the load-bearing problem, which is why Pin is the better default starting point.

## What Do Metaview Reviews Say About Its Sourcing?

Nothing independent. As of September 2026, not one user review of Metaview's AI Sourcing agent appears on G2 or Product Hunt, so teams evaluating it are buying on the demo alone, without [a public track record](/blog/metaview-reviews/). Product Hunt lists Metaview as an AI scribe for recruiting, and its [seven reviews there](https://www.producthunt.com/products/metaview/reviews) (4.3/5) describe the notetaker, where one reviewer called accuracy "good but not infallible."

Whatever criticism exists is concrete:

- **No API, no bulk export.** A July 2026 [Product Hunt review](https://www.producthunt.com/products/metaview/reviews) reads: "We want to integrate deeply with Metaview, but there's no API." The reviewer also wanted bulk access to historical transcripts and summaries for import into their ATS.
- **Sourced results come with a warning label.** Metaview's sourcing documentation says candidates "may occasionally be unavailable, outdated, duplicated, incorrectly matched, or not suitable for the role." Its sourcing guide adds that the data "is not exhaustive, and coverage can vary across locations, industries, and highly specialised roles."
- **The entry plan got smaller at the same price.** Wayback Machine captures show the $100 plan advertising "Unlimited searches on paid plan" on March 6, 2026, and a 200-profile monthly cap by March 11. Unlimited sourcing now costs $300 per user on Max.

Pin's sourcing, by contrast, has its own public record. Customers accept 83% of Pin-recommended candidates into their hiring pipelines (Pin 2026 first-party data). And for the same search-and-outreach work Metaview's agent has no reviews for, Pin holds 4.8/5 on G2, the highest-rated AI recruiting software on G2.

## How Does Pricing Compare?

While Pin sells one plan per seat, Metaview bills every agent separately. Under [Metaview's pricing](/blog/metaview-pricing/), Sourcing, Notetaker, Application Review, Screening, and Reports are each their own line item, so the all-in cost depends on how many agents a team actually runs. Stacked on top are five separate credit meters: sourcing, enrichment, Application Review, Screening, and Notetaker conversations.

| Plan | Pin | Metaview |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free Tier | $0/mo, no credit card | $0, first 100 profiles once (never refresh) |
| Entry | $99/mo Solo (annual billing), unlimited searches | $100/user/mo Pro, capped at 200 profiles/mo |
| Mid | $135/user/mo Professional (annual) or $179 monthly | $300/user/mo Max (unlimited profiles) |
| Top | Business (custom, unlimited seats and contact credits) | Enterprise (custom, demo required) |
| Pricing Model | One plan covers sourcing, outreach, and scheduling | Per-agent: Sourcing, Notetaker ($60/user/mo), Application Review ($150/mo), plus unpriced Screening and Reports |
| Contact Data | 1 credit per email or phone | 3 credits per email, 10 per phone; $10 per 100-credit top-up |
| Self-Serve Signup | Yes | Yes (for Pro and Max tiers) |

Predictability is where Pin's model has the advantage. A recruiter on the Professional plan at $135/user/mo (billed annually) gets talent search, cross-channel sequences, scheduling, and the team inbox in one bill. By contrast, a Metaview customer running Notetaker, AI Sourcing, and AI Reports pays for each agent and adds the Reports add-on at a flat fee that is not published. Third-party spend data from [SpendHound](https://www.spendhound.com/marketplace/metaview-pricing) puts the average Metaview contract at $4,440 a year for enterprises and $3,597 for SMBs, so the published Pro price is the floor, not the ceiling. For five recruiters on unlimited sourcing with 1,000 emails and 250 phone numbers a month, Metaview comes to about $2,000 a month: $1,500 in Max seats plus $500 in credit top-ups. At $675 a month billed annually, Pin Professional covers the same team, with those 1,250 lookups inside the included credits.
