Of the Metaview alternatives built for AI sourcing in 2026, [Pin](https://www.pin.com/) is the best overall, with unlimited candidate searches across 850M+ profiles on paid plans from $99 a month. Metaview now sells each agent as its own line item (Notetaker, Sourcing, Application Review, Screening, and Reports), and this guide covers the sourcing side. That's the product that got more expensive. As late as March 6, 2026, Metaview's $100-per-user sourcing plan advertised unlimited searches, according to a Wayback Machine snapshot of its pricing page. By the March 11, 2026 snapshot, the same $100 bought 200 profiles per user per month, and unlimited profile sourcing had moved to a $300 Max tier.

As the price climbed, so did Metaview's ambitions, well ahead of its track record. The company started as an interview notetaker, launched AI Sourcing only in fall 2025, and announced an autonomous outbound agent, fillmore, that was still waitlist-only when Metaview bought recruiting firm Reval in August 2026 ([Business Wire](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/metaview-acquires-reval-accelerate-shift-130000064.html), August 2026). **Uncapped sourcing now costs three times as much.** As late as March 6, 2026, the pricing page listed AI Sourcing at $100 per user per month with "Unlimited searches on paid plan." By March 11, 2026, the archived page capped the $100 Pro plan at 200 profiles sourced per month, and "Unlimited profiles sourced" had moved to the $300 Max tier. Pro is still sold as "Proactive, 24/7 candidate search," yet the help center reserves scheduled search reruns for Max and Enterprise and warns of a "monthly search limit" on Pro.

**Contact data costs 3 to 10 credits per lookup.** When Metaview launched outreach in February 2026, its announcement said every workspace gets 500 credits per month, an email costs 3 credits, and a phone number costs 10 (Wayback Machine copy, March 6, 2026). That's about 166 emails or 50 phone numbers a month, shared across the entire workspace. Top-ups cost $10 per 100 credits, so each phone number after the free pool costs $1. Enrichment is one of five separate credit meters on the bill. On Pin, one credit unlocks a personal email, a work email, or a phone number.

**Metaview's own docs hedge on the data.** Per its help center, sourced candidates "may occasionally be unavailable, outdated, duplicated, incorrectly matched, or not suitable for the role," and those sourcing credits still count against the cap. Its sourcing guide concedes the data "is not exhaustive," and Metaview doesn't publish how many profiles it can search. Contact data gets the same treatment: the Outreach page promises "verified emails and phone numbers," while the enrichment docs say results "may occasionally be incorrect, outdated, or undeliverable."

**Metaview's fillmore agent has no published price.** That matters because the standard Sourcing agent, per Metaview's own guidance, does not "Automatically contact, progress, or reject any candidate," and its sequences stop when a candidate books through Calendly. Announced in June 2026, fillmore is an autonomous agent that sources, writes outreach, and books screening calls. Its product page says it runs entirely in Slack, with "no web app, no dashboard, no logins." The pitch is "only pay when you hire," yet the per-hire fee isn't published, so you have to book a demo to learn it. In August, when Metaview bought AI-native recruiting firm Reval to speed fillmore up, the agent was still waitlist-only.

**There are no reviews to check any of this against.** Launched only in fall 2025, Metaview's sourcing product has no independent reviews on G2 or Product Hunt, so buyers are going on the demo alone. For the full platform picture, see our [feature-by-feature Pin and Metaview breakdown](/blog/pin-vs-metaview/).

After shipping to thousands of recruiters, one planning mistake keeps coming up: teams size a sourcing tool by open roles instead of by profiles per hire. Pin's 2026 recruiter survey (250+ responses, April to September 2026) asked the question directly. Nearly two in three recruiters (64.5%) source 25 or more candidates for every hire, 38% source 50 or more, and about one in five source 100-plus. A separate question (450+ responses) found half of recruiters carry six or more open roles at once. Among the 100+ recruiters who answered both, 61.6% need more than 200 profiles to work the roles already on their desk, using range midpoints. Even at the low end of every answer, one in four needs 300 or more. If those roles are worked in the same month, that load outruns a Pro seat's 200-profile allowance.

## What Should You Ask Before Buying an AI Sourcing Agent?

Ask five questions before buying an AI sourcing agent: what one profile or hire costs, how much data there is, and how many credit meters stack on the bill. Then ask who approves outreach before it sends and whether the agent works where your team works. Caution is warranted, because Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027), June 2025). Gartner also estimates only about 130 of the thousands of vendors claiming agentic AI are real. Adoption in human resources is early, too: 82% of respondents in McKinsey's 2025 survey said their organizations don't use AI agents in HR at all ([Stanford AI Index 2026](https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_4_economy.pdf)).

1. **What does one profile or one hire cost, in writing?** A pay-per-hire promise without a published fee can't go into a budget.
2. **How much profile data is there, and where are the gaps?** Ask for an index size and coverage by region. Metaview publishes neither and calls its data "not exhaustive."
3. **How many meters does the bill run on?** Metaview runs sourcing, enrichment, Application Review, Screening, and Notetaker on separate credits.
4. **Who approves outreach before it sends?** Recruiter review keeps messages personal, accurate, and on-brand, which matters because only 26% of job applicants trust AI to evaluate them fairly ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them), July 2025).
5. **Does the agent work where your whole team works?** A Slack-only agent with no dashboard is hard to audit across a team.

Pin puts its answers in writing. It publishes its self-serve plan prices and its 850M+ profile database, and it charges one contact credit per email or phone number on a single meter. Recruiters can review each email before it sends or let email steps send automatically, while LinkedIn and SMS steps stay recruiter tasks.

Annually, Metaview Pro and Pin Solo cost almost the same, so the real difference is what each meter counts. Metaview Pro stops at 2,400 sourced profiles a year per user, while Pin Solo includes unlimited candidate searches, two AI agents, and 500 contact credits a month. Workable costs less per sourced candidate than Metaview Pro: once its 3,000 free credits run out, each one costs about $0.19 to $0.24, versus $0.50 per profile on Metaview Pro ($100 divided by 200).

Scale that to a five-recruiter team and the gap widens. Five Pin Professional seats cost $8,100 a year on annual billing, with unlimited searches and 1,000 contact credits per user each month. Five Metaview Max seats cost $18,000 a year before contact data, while five Pro seats ($6,000) cap the whole team at 1,000 profiles a month. Add 1,000 emails and 250 phone numbers a month on Max and the bill reaches about $2,000 a month, or $24,000 a year. Pin Professional covers those 1,250 lookups inside its included credits for $675 a month. Predictable pricing matters more than the entry number once procurement gets involved.

## Where to Start If You're Leaving Metaview Sourcing

Start by exporting the shortlists and ideal candidate profiles you built in Metaview, then rerun the same brief in two finalists for one week. Compare the numbers that determine total cost: qualified profiles returned, the price of a verified email and phone number, and replies earned per sequence. Document the administrative work each tool removes, too, because a cheaper seat that needs manual enrichment and spreadsheet tracking rarely stays cheaper.

Of the Metaview alternatives in this guide, Pin is the one to test first for sourcing-led teams. Its free plan needs no credit card, and recruiters on Pin fill roles in an average of 14 days, the fastest time-to-fill of any AI recruiting platform, per Pin's 2026 user survey.
