## What Do Metaview Reviews Actually Rate?

Metaview's G2 profile held 130 reviews at 4.9 out of 5 in a [June 4, 2025 snapshot of its G2 page](https://web.archive.org/web/20250604013459/https://www.g2.com/products/metaview/reviews), with 124 of those at five stars. By September 2026, G2 listed 132 reviews at 4.8. That's a net gain of two reviews in 15 months.

Those 15 months are exactly when Metaview changed what it sells. Back in June 2025, it raised a [$35M Series B led by GV](https://tech.eu/2025/06/25/metaview-raises-35m-to-replace-hiring-guesswork-with-clarity/), when its product line still centered on the notetaker. Its sourcing page first appears in the Internet Archive on September 16, 2025. Outreach sequences followed in early 2026, then fillmore, an autonomous outbound agent, in June 2026. None of those launches moved the review count.

Metaview's 4.8 comes from its interview notetaker, and nothing in the public record says that score should transfer to a sourcing agent. That gap matters more in 2026 than it used to. Stars earned by an older product describe somebody else's purchase.

### Transcription mistakes and language mix-ups

G2 tags five reviews under Language Limitations. In a February 2025 G2 review, an executive talent consultant who interviews candidates in German wrote that Metaview "mixes up languages" and returned their German interviews as English notes. Another reviewer, a Head of Internal Talent, said the bot sometimes struggles when someone on the call has an accent. Accuracy, in one Product Hunt review, is "good but not infallible," so multilingual teams should budget time to check every summary.

### ATS integration drops and a bot that doesn't show up

One reviewer's integration problem was intermittent, which makes it harder to catch. That same Head of Internal Talent, at a mid-market company, wrote on G2 in November 2024 that the Pinpoint integration "randomly disconnects without warning and will not join/record the interviews it should." A bot that silently misses an interview leaves a gap in the candidate record, and you find out only when the notes aren't there.

### Summaries that still need editing

Writing on G2 in May 2025, a mid-market reviewer said summaries "can feel a bit too generic, requiring manual editing to align with internal evaluation criteria." According to that same reviewer, the tool "occasionally misses context or nuances" in highly technical interviews. An executive search firm owner added that the AI "does not always transcribe information initially on summary document." That's worth weighing if engineering interviews are most of your volume.

### Getting your historical data out

The sharpest recent complaint is a July 2026 Product Hunt review faulting the lack of an API. The reviewer's complaint: "we can't get access to all our historical data from the very beginning." It points at a practical question for any buyer: how do you get your transcripts out if you leave?

## Does Metaview Sourcing Have Independent Reviews Yet?

No. Metaview's sourcing product has been live since fall 2025, yet none of the Metaview reviews on G2 or Product Hunt discuss it. Whatever praise appears on Metaview's sourcing pages is testimonial copy the company chose to publish. Buyers are going on the demo alone, which leaves Metaview's own documentation as the most candid source on how the product behaves.

That documentation hedges on data quality in writing. Per the Sourcing overview (re-checked September 28, 2026), profiles come from "vetted third-party providers," and some candidates "may occasionally be unavailable, outdated, duplicated, incorrectly matched, or not suitable for the role." Because of that, sourcing credits are "non-refundable once used." Its sourcing guide adds that the data "is not exhaustive, and coverage can vary across locations, industries, and highly specialised roles," and Metaview never says how many profiles it searches.

The marketing and the manual disagree on autonomy, too. Metaview sells Sourcing as a "Truly autonomous AI sourcing agent." Its own guidance lists what Sourcing does not do, starting with "Automatically contact, progress, or reject any candidate," and its Sourcing Copilot page says "The agent doesn't refine itself."

Even less of a record exists for Metaview's newest agent. When Metaview announced its Reval acquisition on August 11, 2026, fillmore was still waitlist-only. See our [side-by-side of two AI sourcing agents](/blog/juicebox-vs-metaview/) for how it compares with another AI-first rival.

Built to live entirely in Slack ("no web app, no dashboard, no logins"), fillmore reaches candidates over email, LinkedIn, and WhatsApp, and it promises you'll "only pay when you hire." Metaview hasn't published that per-hire fee, and fillmore's page routes buyers to a demo. With no published fee and no reviews, there's nothing yet to hold Metaview to.

## How Do Profile Caps and Credits Change the Bill?

Metaview publishes sourcing prices, re-checked September 28, 2026: Max costs $300 per user per month for unlimited profiles, Pro costs $100 per user per month for 200 profiles, Free covers your first 100 profiles once, and Enterprise is custom. Contact data sits on top: an email costs 3 credits, a phone number costs 10, and each workspace receives 500 free credits monthly, shared across the entire team. Top-ups run $10 per 100 credits, which prices every phone number at $1 and every email at 30 cents. Our [breakdown of Metaview's per-agent costs](/blog/metaview-pricing/) covers the Notetaker and Application Review plans as well.

Same price, capped product. As late as March 6, 2026, Metaview's $100 sourcing plan advertised "unlimited searches on paid plan," according to an Internet Archive capture of its pricing page. By March 11, a capture of the same page showed the $100 Pro plan capped at 200 sourced profiles a month, with unlimited profiles moved to a new $300 Max tier. Uncapped sourcing now lists at three times the old price. The Pro card still promises "Proactive, 24/7 candidate search," yet Metaview's help center reserves scheduled search reruns for Max and Enterprise and mentions a "monthly search limit" on Pro.

## What Consent Rules Apply to the Metaview Notetaker?

About 11 U.S. states primarily require all-party consent to record a conversation, according to the [Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press](https://www.rcfp.org/introduction-to-reporters-recording-guide/). California, Florida, Illinois, and Washington are on that list. Any interview notetaker, Metaview included, runs straight into those compliance rules.

Metaview's marketing and its contract disagree on who carries that risk. On its privacy page, Metaview says "your compliance responsibilities are covered." Yet its Terms of Service (checked September 2026) say "you are solely responsible for providing any notices to, and obtaining consent from, individuals" on a recording. By default, those recordings, video included, stay for two years on AWS servers in the UK. Pin never records interviews, so there's no recording consent to manage.

Legal risk around notetakers is no longer theoretical. A class action against Otter.ai, filed in August 2025, alleges its notetaker recorded meetings without consent from every participant ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ai-notetaking-tools-under-fire-lessons-otterai-class-action-complaint), 2025). Candidate sentiment adds pressure too, since Greenhouse's [2026 Candidate AI Interview Report](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/63-of-job-seekers-have-faced-an-ai-interview-most-havent-had-a-good-one-yet) found 70% of candidates were never clearly told upfront that AI would evaluate them.

HR teams feel it too. In a Paylocity survey covered by [HR Dive in September 2026](https://www.hrdive.com/news/ai-summaries-leave-a-paper-trail-recruiters-might-not-be-ready-for/830734/), 4 in 5 HR leaders said they were actively managing at least one issue with their AI tools. If notes are your main need, set up [consent rules for recording interviews](/blog/why-record-interviews/) first, and compare Metaview against other [notetakers built for recruiting teams](/blog/best-ai-note-taking-tools-recruiters/).

## How to Read Metaview Reviews Before You Buy

Reviews written before September 2025 describe a different product. Use them for what they are, then fill the gaps with this five-step checklist:

1. **Filter by date.** Treat anything before September 2025 as notetaker feedback only.
2. **Filter by product.** Look for reviews that name Sourcing or fillmore. Right now, G2 and Product Hunt have none.
3. **Ask for sourcing references.** Two customers who've used AI Sourcing for 90+ days will tell you more than 130 notetaker reviews.
4. **Model your credit burn.** Multiply profiles by your email and phone mix, then subtract the shared 500 free credits.
5. **Run a side-by-side trial.** Send the same requisition through each tool and compare acceptance, not volume.
