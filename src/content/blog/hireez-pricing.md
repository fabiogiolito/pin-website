# hireEZ Pricing 2026: How Much hireEZ Costs, Free Trial, Plans, and Alternatives

hireEZ pricing typically runs $169 to $199 per recruiter license per month for entry plans, climbing past $250 for enterprise, with a $13,000 median annual contract across [Vendr's anonymized buyer data](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/hireez) (2026). By contrast, [Pin](https://www.pin.com/) publishes its rates openly and starts at $99 per month (billed annually) with a free tier and no credit card. Real-world hireEZ deals span $6,600 to $25,000 yearly, with high-end accounts reaching roughly $48,000. There is no public hireEZ price page, no self-serve free plan, just a 14-day sales-gated trial.

Pin ships the largest multi-source candidate database in the industry: 850M+ profiles spanning professional networks, GitHub, Stack Overflow, patents, and academic publications. This guide walks through every cost component hireEZ buyers pay for. Topics covered: per-license rates, contact-credit overages, setup fees, contract minimums, and the renewal-trap pattern that quietly turns a $13K deal into $20K by year three. We compare hireEZ to five alternatives, including LinkedIn Recruiter, [SeekOut](/blog/seekout-pricing/), [Gem](/blog/gem-pricing/), Fetcher, and Pin.

## How Much Does hireEZ Cost?

Most hireEZ buyers pay between $6,600 and $25,000 a year, against a $13,000 median annual contract in Vendr marketplace data verified August 2026. Per seat, three license bands govern hireEZ in 2026, from roughly $169 to $250+ per user per month. Nobody advertises those numbers. Searching for hireEZ official pricing turns up no rate card, because the company publishes none on its website. Every confirmed dollar figure in this article comes from third-party software marketplaces (Vendr, Software Finder, AvaHR) and aggregated user reports. The platform follows a per-recruiter-license model with annual billing and a contact-credit allotment bolted on.

Here is the per-license math from current marketplace estimates:

Some larger deployments climb to $48,000 a year. That spread tells you what's really happening at the negotiating table: per-license rates are the anchor, but credit packs, integrations, and seat minimums quickly multiply the bill.

## What Plans Does hireEZ Offer?

Three named plans plus a sales-gated trial: Startups (~$169/user/mo, 100 monthly credits), Professional (~$199/user/mo, ~4,000 monthly credits), and Enterprise ($250+/user/mo, custom). Each plan scales on three axes: number of recruiter licenses, monthly contact-reveal credits, and which AI features unlock.

### Startups Plan (~$169 per user per month)

The entry band targets solo recruiters and small groups of up to three sourcers. Each license includes roughly 100 contact-reveal credits monthly. That sounds workable until you do the math: 100 reveals on a typical sourcer running 15-20 active reqs is just 5-7 candidate contacts per role, before any reply or fallout. Run sequences with multi-step outreach and a Startups license is dry by mid-month. There is no rollover, and overages require a custom credit pack negotiated with sales.

### Professional Plan (~$199 per user per month)

Mid-band pricing for growing in-house groups. Credit allotments expand to roughly 4,000 monthly per organization on annual deals. AI candidate matching turns on, and outreach sequences (email, SMS, and LinkedIn via the Chrome extension) come standard. Most groups that go past a single sourcer land here. Annual billing is required for the published rate, and discounts on multi-year terms are available but require a 2-3 year commit.

### Enterprise Plan ($250+ per user per month, custom)

Volume-based pricing kicks in for groups of 5-10 licenses or more. Enterprise unlocks advanced DEI filters, dedicated customer success, custom integrations, and bespoke credit allowances. According to the [Vendr buyer guide](https://www.vendr.com/buyer-guides/hireez), Enterprise contracts start near $7,000 yearly for small deployments and can reach $48,000+ for global teams with multiple ATS integrations.

### Free Trial Status

hireEZ historically offered a "Free Forever" plan with limited credits. As of 2026, that plan is effectively deprecated. hireEZ now routes prospects to a sales-led demo and a short, gated trial with a small allotment of contact reveals. There is no self-serve sign-up. Compare that to Pin's free tier, which requires no credit card and gives recruiters access to the full search and outreach experience inside a real workspace, not a demo sandbox.

## What's the Total Cost of Ownership?

Five cost components stack on top of the per-license bill. Together they often add 30-60% to the sticker before year one closes. Per-user math undersells the budget line. Here is what gets layered on.

### Contact-Credit Overages

Reveal a candidate's email or phone and the meter ticks down. Hit zero and sourcing stops. You either buy more or wait for the next billing cycle. Top-up packs cost what the account team quotes; they don't publish the rate. The practical effect: the listed user price is a floor, never a ceiling, especially when sourcers run dozens of reqs at once.

### How Many Reqs Does a Recruiter Actually Run at Once?

Pin's own product data shows why a small monthly allowance breaks down in practice (Pin product data, 900+ recruiters, 12 months to July 2026). Across 900+ recruiters tracked over 12 months, the busiest month carried twice as many actively sourced reqs as a typical month, a median of 6 at peak against 3 normally. The split is stark: 69.1% hit at least one month sourcing against 5 or more reqs, yet only 31.9% carry 5+ in a typical month. At 100 reveals per license, hireEZ's Startups plan leaves about 16 contacts per req in a six-req peak month. Overage packs are then quoted case by case, which is exactly what makes hireEZ pricing hard to forecast a year out. Lookups are metered on Pin too. What differs is headroom: Solo includes 500 a month and Professional 1,000 per user, five to ten times the Startups allowance, while Business is unlimited. Every email or phone lookup costs a flat 1 credit, while [some rivals charge 3 to 10 credits per phone number](/blog/juicebox-alternatives/).

### Fees to Set Up the Platform

Per the [Vendr buyer guide](https://www.vendr.com/buyer-guides/hireez), onboarding fees run "a few thousand dollars to significant five-figure amounts" depending on how complex it is to wire up your existing stack. [Hirevire's review aggregator](https://hirevire.com/blog/hireez-reviews-pricing-alternatives) cites $1,000-$2,500 for simple setups and higher when you need to connect Workday, Greenhouse, or Bullhorn.

### Contract Minimums and Auto-Renewal

You commit annually to access published pricing. Enterprise plans commonly require 5-10 user minimums. Contracts include auto-renewal clauses with a 60- to 90-day window to cancel, surfaced in multiple Hirevire-aggregated user reviews. Miss it and your contract rolls over for another 12 months, often at a higher rate.

### Renewal Escalators

The single most underreported cost component. Hirevire and Vendr both describe 20-30% year-over-year price hikes at renewal when buyers negotiate no cap. It repeats. One documented case required a 2-year recommit just to keep year-2 pricing flat. Model the math at 25%: a $13,000 contract becomes $16,250 in year 2 and $20,313 in year 3. That's a 56% lift over three years on the same product. Sources: [Hirevire](https://hirevire.com/blog/hireez-reviews-pricing-alternatives) (2025) and the [Vendr buyer guide](https://www.vendr.com/buyer-guides/hireez).

### Recruiter Onboarding Time

Multiple [G2 reviewers](https://www.g2.com/products/hireez) flag the interface as "feature-dense" with a steep learning curve. Budget 2-4 weeks of recruiter ramp before sourcers produce real output at the per-license rate you signed for. That ramp costs real money. ## What Hidden Costs Should You Expect?

Three patterns recur in user reports beyond the line items already covered. The pricing playbook hireEZ uses (no public rate card, sales-led trial, custom credit packs, multi-tier add-ons) mirrors how [Eightfold AI's enterprise contracts](/blog/eightfold-pricing/) and most legacy enterprise sourcing platforms operate. It maximizes revenue per buyer. It also compresses budget visibility for the buying team.

**Add-on modules priced separately.** Agentic AI, [launched March 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hireez-unveils-agentic-ai-a-semi-autonomous-approach-to-smarter-more-strategic-recruiting-302411200.html), and ResumeSense, [launched November 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hireez-introduces-resumesense-to-detect-ai-resume-manipulation-and-protect-ethical-hiring-302610392.html), are positioned as differentiators in marketing materials. Whether they're bundled in the base plan or sold as add-ons varies by deal; verify before you sign.

**ATS connector setup.** Custom hooks into Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, or Bullhorn often require separate professional-services engagements. Quoted as part of "implementation" but billed as a distinct line item.

**Credit overage opacity.** Blow through your monthly contact-reveal cap and top-up packs get negotiated case by case. No published rate exists. Power-users end up paying more than light-users for the same data.

## How Do You Negotiate hireEZ Pricing?

Three negotiating levers consistently work for buyers in [Vendr's transaction data](https://www.vendr.com/buyer-guides/hireez):

1. **Negotiate a renewal cap up front.** Ask for a written 5-8% annual increase cap as a contract clause. Without it, you're exposed to the 20-30% renewal pattern.
2. **Lock multi-year for flat pricing.** A 2-year commit at year-1 pricing is a common concession; a 3-year commit at a 5-10% discount is achievable for enterprise deals.
3. **Push for credit overage clarity.** Get the per-credit overage rate in writing before signing. The opaque "negotiate when you run out" model is what blows budget late in the year.

End of fiscal quarter (March, June, September, December) is the strongest timing window.
