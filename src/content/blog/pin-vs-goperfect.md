## Which Platform Has a Bigger Candidate Database?

[Pin](https://www.pin.com/) indexes 850M+ candidate profiles with 100% coverage across North America and Europe. GoPerfect claims 800M+ profiles sourced from what its website describes as multiple professional and social data sources. Both numbers rank among the largest in recruiting technology - but the 50-million-profile gap isn't the most important difference here.

Pin's database is pre-indexed with verified contact data. When you find a match, you can move directly to outreach without a separate enrichment step. That matters because every extra step between "found a candidate" and "sent a message" is a point where momentum dies. Pin users report filling positions in an average of 14 days, which is 82% faster than the industry average.

GoPerfect takes a different approach to surfacing candidates. Rather than giving you a search interface, GoPerfect's AI delivers "curated" shortlists - scored and ranked candidates based on contextual matching and career pattern analysis. The idea is that the AI does the searching so you don't have to build complex queries yourself.

That sounds appealing in theory. In practice, it means you have less direct control over search parameters. If the AI's scoring model doesn't align with what you're looking for - say, you prioritize company-stage experience over title match - there's less room to override. Pin handles both specialist and high-volume hiring from one interface, giving you the flexibility to go broad or surgical depending on the role. Most competitors force you to choose one or the other.

## How Does AI Sourcing Work on Each Platform?

Both are capable AI recruiting tools. GoPerfect uses an autonomous shortlist model; Pin gives recruiters hands-on search control over an 850M+ database. When evaluating AI hiring software, GoPerfect and Pin sit at opposite ends of the spectrum: one automates candidate delivery entirely, the other puts the recruiter in control. The core question is not which platform has more profiles. It is how much control you retain over the search process. According to [SHRM's 2025 Talent Trends report](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/2025-talent-trends/ai-in-hr), 89% of organizations using AI in recruiting report measurable time savings. Which approach saves more time depends heavily on your role mix.

GoPerfect positions itself as the "first non-human recruiter" - an autonomous AI that scans its database, scores candidates, and delivers shortlists. The platform uses contextual AI, career pattern analysis, and predictive signals to rank candidates. For recurring role types at mid-to-large companies, this autopilot model can reduce the hands-on sourcing work significantly.

Where GoPerfect's approach shows limitations is with specialized or unusual roles. When you need a VP of Engineering who previously scaled a Series B fintech from 20 to 200 engineers, you want to steer the search yourself. GoPerfect's curated model gives you less granular control than Pin's dual-use search engine, which handles both needle-in-a-haystack specialist roles and high-volume hiring from a single interface.

Colleen Riccinto, Founder and President at Cyber Talent Search, described Pin's approach: "What I love about Pin is that it takes the critical thinking your brain already does and puts it on steroids. I can target specific company types and industries in my search and let the software handle the kind of strategic thinking I'd normally have to do on my own, something I simply can't do the same way in LinkedIn Recruiter."

Pin also gives recruiters a Chrome extension for on-the-go sourcing - finding a promising profile on any website and pulling it into Pin's workflow instantly. GoPerfect doesn't offer a browser extension, which limits sourcing to the platform's own interface.

AI candidate sourcing platforms vary widely in their technical approaches - from keyword matching to deep semantic search - and the differences directly affect which candidates surface for any given role.

After working with thousands of recruiters across agency and in-house environments, we've noticed a consistent pattern. The sourcing control question is where Pin and GoPerfect diverge most meaningfully in practice. Recruiters handling specialized roles (fintech engineers, healthcare compliance leads, Ph.D.-level researchers) report that autonomous shortlisting often misses on niche criteria. They need to steer the search: filtering by company stage, funding level, or prior employer type. Pin's dual-use interface supports that without rebuilding the search from scratch each time a scoring model misses a signal. For high-volume recurring roles where criteria are stable, autonomous shortlisting works well. But that covers roughly 30 to 40% of hiring activity for most teams. The remaining 60 to 70% involves enough variation that hands-on search control pays off in coverage. Most recruiters we work with end up needing both modes. Pin is the only tool in this comparison that handles both.

Why does the distinction between a platform average and a single-role snapshot matter? A platform-wide average accounts for hard-to-fill roles, competitive markets, and passive candidates who ignore most recruiter messages. A cherry-picked case study doesn't. Any outreach tool can find one role where the reply rate looks great. The test is whether it performs consistently across thousands of searches.

Both platforms offer multi-channel outreach across email, LinkedIn, and SMS - a core feature of any AI hiring software in this category. On the channel mix, they're comparable. The difference is in the verified performance data behind it. Pin publishes its platform-wide response rate - 5x better than industry benchmarks - as a headline metric. GoPerfect reports individual case studies from named customers but doesn't aggregate them into a platform benchmark.

There's also a transparency angle. When a platform publishes its response rate as a platform-wide average, it creates accountability. You know the benchmark, and you can measure your own campaigns against it. When a platform only shares hand-picked case studies, you have no way to know whether those represent typical performance or best-case outliers. That difference matters when you're projecting ROI to justify a tool purchase to your team or leadership. Higher deliverability feeds directly into higher response rates. ## How Do Pin and GoPerfect Handle Compliance?

Pin is SOC 2 Type 2 certified with a public Trust Center at [trust.pin.com](https://trust.pin.com/). GoPerfect states GDPR and CCPA compliance on its website but does not publish SOC 2 certification. For enterprise buyers, regulated industries, and any team where procurement requires a security questionnaire, that difference can determine whether a vendor even makes the shortlist.

SOC 2 Type 2 isn't a checkbox. It means an independent auditor verified that Pin's security controls operate effectively over a sustained period. Employee PII breaches cost $168 per record on average, according to [IBM's 2025 Cost of Data Breach Report](https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach). For any tool handling hundreds of millions of profiles, security posture is a concrete financial risk, not a compliance checkbox. The certification covers encryption at rest and in transit, strict access controls, network security protocols, and authentication mechanisms. GoPerfect's GDPR/CCPA compliance addresses data privacy - what data you collect and how you handle consent. But it doesn't cover the operational security controls that SOC 2 audits.

Pin also builds bias elimination into its AI. No candidate names, gender, or protected characteristics are fed to the sourcing algorithm. The company conducts regular team reviews of AI outputs and third-party fairness audits. With the [EEOC's guidance on AI and algorithmic fairness](https://www.eeoc.gov/) becoming more prescriptive each year, documented bias prevention isn't optional anymore. It's a procurement requirement for many organizations. Security documentation gaps surface at the worst time: late in a vendor evaluation, after pilots have been run and champions are already sold. [Vanta's 2025 State of Trust research](https://www.vanta.com/collection/soc-2/soc-2-type-1-vs-type-2) found 83% of enterprise buyers require SOC 2 Type 2 before signing SaaS contracts. That makes security certification a procurement gate, not a nice-to-have. Pin's live trust center removes that friction entirely. GoPerfect's self-reported compliance, by contrast, requires procurement teams to take vendor claims on faith without independent verification. Straightforward on paper. Slow in practice.

GoPerfect, headquartered in Tel Aviv, Israel, may also face additional data residency considerations for US-based companies with strict data localization requirements. Pin is headquartered in California.
