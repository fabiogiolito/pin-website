Time-to-hire benchmarks for 2026 start earlier than most teams measure: the median candidate replies to recruiter cold outreach in 3.9 days, per a sample of 230,000+ outreach threads on [Pin](https://www.pin.com). Reaching a first interview takes a median of 5.8 days from the very first message. Pin, the AI recruiting platform trusted by 2,000+ organizations and 20,000+ users, measured both clocks across 17 role families, and the dispersion is wider than the aggregate medians suggest. Customer Success candidates reply in a median 2.9 days, while recruiters being recruited take 6.0.

That upstream leg is invisible in every public benchmark. [Ashby](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/recruiting-operations-benchmarks-talent-trends) starts its 2026 clock at application. [NACE](https://www.naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/trends-and-predictions/employers-make-more-offers-as-student-acceptance-rate-rises-response-time-shortens) measures interview-to-offer. And the only by-role timing study anyone still cites, Glassdoor's interview-duration research, dates to 2017. Gem's 2026 benchmarks show sourced candidates are nearly 8x more likely to be hired than inbound applicants, yet nobody publishes how long that sourcing leg takes. This report fills the gap with first-party data. It then lines those numbers up against the public benchmarks from Ashby, NACE, Indeed Hiring Lab, and Employ, so you can benchmark your entire funnel, not just the back half.

## How Fast Do Candidates Reply to Cold Outreach?

Candidates reply to recruiter cold outreach in a median of 3.9 days, based on Pin data covering 230,000+ outreach threads from January 2023 through June 2026. At the fast end, a quarter of replies land inside a single day (p25 = 0.96 days). On the long tail, a quarter take more than 7.9 days, and 10% of eventual responses arrive after 16.8 days.

Underneath that median, the distribution tells a considerably sharper story. Only 4.7% of replies arrive within the first hour. Roughly 1 in 4 (26%) land within 24 hours, and by 48 hours you've heard from approximately a third of everyone who will ever respond. Day seven effectively closes the window: 70.8% of all replies have already arrived by then, and 90% by day 17.

What does that mean in practice? Simply this: the first week is the whole game.

By day seven, more than 7 in 10 of the replies a campaign will ever receive have already arrived; fewer than 3 in 10 trickle in afterward. Recruiting teams that wait two weeks before following up aren't being patient; they're forfeiting the window in which interest actually exists. Exactly this logic is why [a complete guide to outreach automation](/blog/automated-candidate-outreach/) treats follow-up timing as a core design decision rather than an afterthought.

> **Bottom line:**
>
> - **Median reply takes 3.9 days.** Across 230,000+ Pin outreach threads, a quarter of candidates answer within a day, and 70.8% of all replies land inside a week.
> - **Role family moves the number by more than 2x.** Customer Success candidates reply in a median 2.9 days; recruiter-on-recruiter outreach takes 6.0 days.
> - **First interview follows fast.** The median candidate who interviews does so 5.8 days after first contact, and 58.9% interview within a week.
> - **Public benchmarks miss this leg entirely.** Ashby, NACE, and Employ all start the clock at application or later, so teams benchmarking only time-to-fill never see their slowest stage.

## How Do Reply and Interview Times Vary by Role Family?

Reply speed varies by more than 2x across role families, from a 2.9-day median for Customer Success and Support candidates to 6.0 days when recruiters reach out to other recruiters. These time-to-reply benchmarks come from classifying the 230,000+ outreach threads by role family, then computing medians, quartiles, and 24-hour reply shares for every family with a defensible sample. The final column adds each family's median days from first outreach to first interview.

Engineering (non-software) cleared the sample bar for reply timing but not for interview timing, so its interview cell reads n/a. An unmistakable pattern emerges from the table. Families that reply fastest (Customer Success, Product, Marketing) are communication-heavy roles where checking messages is the job. Slowest are exactly the families where demand runs hottest: Software Engineering (4.1 days), Data and AI (4.2), and Nursing and Healthcare (4.9). Because these candidates field the heaviest volume of recruiter attention, each individual message waits longer in the queue.

Sitting at the very bottom: recruiters themselves, at a 6.0-day median. The people who send outreach for a living take the longest to answer it.

Pin's take: the p75 column matters more than the median for capacity planning, and it's the column most teams never examine. A Software Engineering search where a quarter of replies take 9+ days behaves completely differently from a Marketing search where p75 is 6.8. We watch teams build follow-up cadences as if every role behaves like the average, then wonder why their engineering pipelines feel slow. They aren't slow; they're operating on a different clock. Teams that internalize this set role-specific expectations with hiring managers up front: a week of silence on a Data/AI thread is normal, not a dead lead. Escalating it as one erodes the hiring manager's trust in every subsequent status update. In-demand candidates frequently reply on day 8 or 9, so sequences that stop after two touches in the first five days quietly abandon a meaningful share of eventual responders. We observe the identical asymmetry across agencies and in-house teams alike, irrespective of headcount or industry vertical.

## How Long From Outreach to First Interview?

Across a sample of 19,000+ candidates in Pin's data who progressed from cold contact to a scheduled interview, the median journey from first outreach message to first interview runs 5.8 days. Nearly 6 in 10 (58.9%) interview within a week of first contact, and 79.7% within two weeks.

Interview timing also compresses the role-family spread considerably. Where reply medians ranged from 2.9 to 6.0 days, interview medians cluster between 4.5 and 7.0 days, meaning every role family advances at approximately the same velocity once a candidate engages.

Healthcare sits at the slow end of both clocks (4.9 days to reply, 7.0 to interview), which fits what every healthcare recruiter already knows. Clinical talent is the most courted talent in the market, and shift schedules make interview logistics genuinely harder. Identical dynamics govern recruiter-on-recruiter hiring at 6.2 days. Longest-courtship families are the highest-demand ones, not the least interested ones.

Notice what these two clocks imply when you stack them. Median reply: 3.9 days; median interview: 5.8.

For candidates who engage, the reply IS most of the journey, because once someone answers, the transition from conversation to scheduled interview consumes only a couple of days. So the bottleneck in the outreach-to-interview leg isn't scheduling; it's waiting for the initial response, and how aggressively you follow up during that window. That has direct consequences for [how candidates move through each hiring stage](/blog/recruitment-funnel-benchmarks/) downstream, since a pipeline that engages candidates inside the first week starts every later stage with fresher, warmer talent.

## What Separates Time-to-Fill, Time-to-Hire, and Time-to-Reply?

Time-to-fill measures days from requisition approval to offer acceptance, while time-to-hire measures days from a candidate entering your pipeline to accepting. Time-to-reply, the metric this study adds, measures days from first cold outreach to first candidate response. Three clocks, three diagnoses, and [SHRM's calculation guidance](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/tools/forms/time-to-hire-time-to-fill-calculation-spreadsheet) treats the first two as separate metrics for a reason.

Here's how the full timeline assembles:

| Metric | Clock starts | Clock ends | What it diagnoses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time-to-reply | First outreach message | First candidate response | Outreach quality, follow-up cadence, role demand |
| Time to first interview | First outreach message | First interview | Top-of-funnel velocity end to end |
| Time-to-hire | Candidate enters pipeline | Offer accepted | Funnel speed and process friction |
| Time-to-fill | Requisition approved | Offer accepted | Whole-org process: approvals, capacity, collaboration |
| Time-to-start | Offer accepted | First day on the job | Notice periods, background checks, onboarding |

Every glossary page that ranks for these terms, including [AIHR's time-to-hire guide](https://www.aihr.com/blog/time-to-hire/) with its formulas, shares one blind spot. They start counting when a candidate applies. Yet for sourced candidates, who Gem's 2026 benchmark data shows are nearly 8x more likely to be hired than inbound applicants, the journey starts days or weeks before any application exists. A team that only tracks time-to-fill can't see whether its slowest stage is req approval, outreach response, or interview scheduling. Teams that track all five clocks, alongside [the dashboard metrics recruiting leaders watch](/blog/recruiter-kpis-dashboard/), can.

## How Do These Numbers Compare to Public Time-to-Hire Benchmarks?

Pin's 5.8-day outreach-to-interview median sits dramatically upstream of the public time-to-hire benchmarks, which is the point. Ashby's 2026 Talent Trends Report (54M+ applications) puts the median time to first fill at 60 days for business roles and 75 days for technical roles. [SHRM's 2026 recruiting benchmarks](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/recruiting-benchmarking) put median time-to-fill for nonexecutive positions at 39 calendar days. And [Employ's 2026 hiring benchmarks](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/07/3214475/0/en/what-will-good-hiring-look-like-in-2026-new-employ-inc-report-reveals-key-recruiting-benchmarks.html) report time-to-fill falling to 63.5 days in 2025, from 67.7 days in 2024.

## Why the First Week Decides the Hire

The first week decides the hire because response odds decay with every day a thread sits unanswered. Recruiting follows the same speed-to-contact curve that sales teams have documented for years. Dr. James Oldroyd's 2007 Lead Response Management study with InsideSales.com found that calling a lead within 5 minutes rather than 30 made contact roughly 100x more likely. His 2011 follow-up in [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found that firms responding within an hour were nearly 7x as likely to qualify a lead as those that waited even an hour longer.

Recruiting's decay curve runs in days rather than minutes, but it's just as real. Ashby's 2026 report found candidate NPS holds steady through about day 9 of a process, then drops sharply after the three-week mark. Meanwhile, Pin's distribution data puts hard numbers on the front of that curve: 70.8% of replies arrive within 7 days, so every day of delayed follow-up burns a measurable slice of the remaining response probability.

Folklore tells a blunter version: "the best candidates are off the market in 10 days," a claim most often traced to a 2016 ERE column by Dr. John Sullivan, which credits Officevibe. Neither published an underlying dataset. Real numbers are both more forgiving and more demanding. Forgiving, because replies keep trickling in past day 10 (the p90 is 16.8 days). Demanding, because the median candidate who will interview has already done so by day 6. Wait ten days to follow up and you haven't lost everyone, but you've let the most responsive candidates, the ones most likely to convert, go cold.

## Methodology

This study draws on a sample of 230,000+ recruiter outreach threads and 19,000+ candidates who progressed from cold outreach to a first interview, all from Pin's platform data (January 2023 to June 2026). Reply timing measures how long a candidate takes to respond after a sequence sends its first outreach. Interview timing runs from the first message sent to whichever comes first: a scheduled interview event or the moment the candidate moves into an interviewing stage. We report medians and quartiles rather than averages, since long-tail outliers (0.65% of threads exceed 90 days) distort means. Role families were classified from job titles; families with statistically thin samples were excluded rather than published. All figures reflect aggregate candidate behavior observed across Pin's customer base, not the performance of any single team.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a good time to hire in 2026?

Public 2026 benchmarks put a typical hire between roughly 39 and 75 days from requisition. SHRM's 2026 benchmarking finds a 39-day median time-to-fill for nonexecutive positions. Ashby's 2026 Talent Trends Report shows a median 60 days to first fill for business roles and 75 for technical roles. Beating those medians means compressing the top of the funnel. Pin data shows candidates who interview do so a median of 5.8 days after first outreach, so the biggest gains come before the application ever exists.

### What is the difference between time to fill and time to hire?

Time-to-fill counts days from requisition approval to offer acceptance and diagnoses the whole organizational process. Time-to-hire counts days from a candidate entering the pipeline to acceptance and diagnoses funnel speed. Employ's 2026 report put time-to-fill at 63.5 days in 2025; time-to-hire runs shorter because it skips req approval and sourcing lag.

### How long does it take candidates to respond to recruiter outreach?

The median candidate replies to recruiter cold outreach in 3.9 days, according to Pin data from 230,000+ outreach threads. About 26% reply within 24 hours and 70.8% within a week. Reply speed varies by role: Customer Success candidates answer in a median 2.9 days, while healthcare candidates take 4.9.

### How does time-to-hire vary by role and seniority?

Seniority and role type both stretch the clock, and they compound. Ashby's 2026 data shows senior roles take 37% longer to fill than junior ones (a median 71 days versus 52), and technical roles take about 15 days longer than business roles. At the top of the funnel, Pin data shows executive candidates reply to cold outreach in a median 3.7 days and reach a first interview in 6.0, close to the overall pattern.

### How can recruiters reduce time to hire?

Attack the waiting, not the interviewing. Follow up within the first week, when 70.8% of all replies arrive, and automate scheduling so replies convert to interviews in days.
