In the Pin vs Noon matchup, Pin is the better AI recruiting agent for most hiring teams: it does more of the work, and it does it more precisely. Pin's AI agent sources 24/7 across 850M+ profiles with 100% North American and European coverage. It runs outreach sequences with 5x better response rates than industry averages, books interviews on a built-in scheduler, and publishes its prices, starting with a free plan. Noon, by contrast, won't tell you its price until a sales call, shares its SOC 2 report only under NDA, and publishes no customer case study.

The AI recruiting market reached $752 million in 2026 and is expected to hit $1.2 billion by 2033, according to [SkyQuest's AI Recruitment Market report](https://www.skyquestt.com/report/ai-recruitment-market). With 43% of companies now using AI for HR tasks - nearly doubled from 26% a year earlier, per [SHRM's 2025 Talent Trends report](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/2025-talent-trends/ai-in-hr) - the question isn't whether to adopt an AI recruiting platform. It's which one to pick.

This head-to-head comparison breaks down Pin and Noon across seven categories: database coverage, AI sourcing, outreach automation, interview scheduling, pricing, compliance, and agency support. Every section includes verifiable data so you can make the call yourself, and one section corrects the claims Noon's own comparison pages make about Pin.

> **TL;DR:**
>
> - **Agent vs agent, Pin does more.** Pin's AI agent works 24/7 in Assist or Autopilot mode over 850M+ pre-indexed profiles and books interviews on its own scheduler, no Calendly duct-tape required. Noon crawls the open web, publishes no database size, and books through a Calendly integration.
> - **No price until a sales call.** Pin publishes a free plan, Solo at $99/mo billed annually, and Professional at $135 per user per month. Noon publishes no dollar price anywhere, and its 10x guarantee requires an annual contract, pays refunds after 12 months, and caps at $1,000,000.
> - **Little customer proof.** Noon has no Capterra or Product Hunt listing and no published case study, and the big-brand tiles on its homepage link to podcast episodes. Pin is rated 4.8/5 on G2, the highest-rated AI recruiting software on G2.
> - **Security you can check.** Pin's SOC 2 Type II status, controls, and subprocessor list are public on trust.pin.com, and uptime is public on status.pin.com. Noon has neither and shares its report only under NDA.
> - **5x better response rates vs. undisclosed.** Pin's sequences pair automated email with LinkedIn and SMS tasks and deliver 5x better response rates than industry averages. Noon documents email and SMS only and publishes no benchmark.

## Which Platform Has a Bigger Candidate Database?

[Pin](https://www.pin.com/) has the larger database by a wide margin: 850M+ indexed profiles with 100% coverage across North America and Europe. Noon has no published database size figure - it searches the open web in real time instead. The median time to fill a nonexecutive role is 39 days, according to [SHRM's 2026 recruiting benchmarking](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/recruiting-benchmarking), and database coverage is one of the biggest factors in reducing that number.

Every one of those 850M+ profiles is searchable and contactable directly from the platform, with no hopping between tools - the largest multi-source candidate database in the industry. No tabs to juggle.

Noon AI takes a fundamentally different approach. Its AI searches across the open web - LinkedIn, GitHub, Reddit, personal sites, and other public sources. That sounds broad, but it introduces a trade-off: you can't verify the exact size or coverage of what you're searching. For niche roles where candidates have minimal online presence, web-scraping approaches can miss profiles that a full-coverage, pre-indexed database would catch. Coverage gaps cost candidates.

That wasn't always Noon's pitch. In a [June 2026 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20260618173652/https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/137-noon-vs-seekout) of its SeekOut comparison, Noon described both tools as "AI-powered sourcing platforms with 800M+ profile databases." Today, its Pin comparison page says "Pin's search is a database" as if that were a flaw.

Does database size actually affect outcomes? Recruiters using Pin fill positions in an average of 14 days - 82% faster than traditional methods, according to Pin's 2026 user survey. When your database covers 850M+ people, the AI has more candidates to evaluate. More candidates in, better matches out.

Here's what surprised us while building Pin: for recruiters, database consistency matters as much as database size. A web crawl returns whatever it happens to reach that day, so the same search can surface a different shortlist on Monday than on Thursday, with no way to tell which one was complete.

That inconsistency creates a hidden cost. Recruiters end up running searches multiple times to feel confident in the coverage, adding hours of verification back into a process that's supposed to save time. Pin cuts manual sourcing time by 90%, per Pin's 2026 user survey - a number that only holds if the underlying database is stable and pre-indexed. For high-volume hiring - 10+ open roles simultaneously - that variance compounds into a meaningful productivity gap. Recruiters using Pin fill positions in an average of 14 days because they trust the first search, not the third. Consistency compounds.

## How Does AI Sourcing Differ Between Pin and Noon?

Pin searches a proprietary database of 850M+ pre-indexed profiles; Noon crawls the open web autonomously. That's the core difference, and it shapes everything from search consistency to contact data availability. Eighty-four percent of talent leaders plan to use AI sourcing in 2026, per [Korn Ferry's talent acquisition trends report](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/featured-topics/talent-recruitment/talent-acquisition-trends) - so choosing the right approach matters now.

From a single platform, Pin's AI handles both needle-in-a-haystack specialist roles (think "series-B fintech CFO with APAC experience") and high-volume hiring (50+ nurses in a metro area). Most competitors force you to choose one approach or the other. Pin doesn't.

Noon AI sourcing relies on web crawling at query time. Its AI scans LinkedIn, GitHub, Reddit, Slack communities, and personal websites, and hiring managers calibrate each role with thumbs-up and thumbs-down feedback. That calibration is slow to start, and Noon has said as much: its November 2025 comparison listed "Not instantly precise without training" among Noon's own cons. Noon's buyer's guide ([June 2026 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20260618174210/https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/06-ai-recruitment-platforms-compared)) says match accuracy "typically reaches 70-80%+ positive signal rates" only after 20-30 reviews. So the first couple dozen candidates on every new role are calibration, and even the target leaves 20-30% of matches earning a thumbs-down. Underneath, the search still depends on how visible a candidate is online at the moment the crawl runs.

Where does this difference show up in practice? Pin's structured database means every search is consistent and repeatable. Search for "senior backend engineers in Austin with 5+ years of Python experience" today and you'll get a complete result set. Run the same search next week and you'll get the same coverage plus any new profiles. Repeatability. Web-scraping approaches, by contrast, can produce different results each time depending on indexing lag, site access, and profile availability.

### Agent vs Agent: Which AI Does More, and More Precisely?

Pin's AI agent does more of the recruiting work than Noon's, and it does that work more precisely. In Autopilot mode, Pin's agent sources around the clock, scores every candidate with a one-line reason you can audit, sends email sequences, follows up, and books interviews against your calendar. In Assist mode, you approve each step. Either way, it calibrates to your bar from every candidate you accept or pass on. The recruiter sets the bar; the agent does the searching, ranking, email outreach, and booking.

Noon markets its AI as a [fully autonomous recruiting agent](/blog/juicebox-vs-noon/) too. Yet its own [Greenhouse setup guide](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/30498665107227-Noon-integration), last updated October 2024, has the AI send "a new batch of candidates for your review" every day, and emails go out only after you confirm your verdicts. The bigger difference is what each agent runs on. Without a structured database to search, Noon's agent makes judgment calls about which online profiles are relevant, current, and contactable, and those calls can vary run to run. Autonomy without precision just produces a longer list to review.

Precision is where the foundation shows. Pin's agent works from pre-indexed, verified profiles, so it never has to guess whether a candidate's information is current; that verification happened during indexing. According to Pin's 2026 user survey, 83% of the candidates Pin recommends are accepted into hiring pipelines - the highest acceptance rate in the industry. That reliability is why recruiters trust the agent to run at scale.

Noon AI doesn't publish a comparable candidate acceptance rate. Without that benchmark, it's hard to evaluate whether Noon's agent is finding the right people or just finding people. For teams that need predictable, auditable sourcing results - especially in regulated industries - the distinction matters. Predictability is the point.

Contact data is another advantage. Because profiles are pre-indexed with verified email addresses and phone numbers, recruiters move from "found a match" to "sent outreach" in minutes. That's not a given. With web-based sourcing, contact information often requires a separate enrichment step - adding cost and latency before outreach can even begin. Pin's contact lookups cost one credit per email or phone number, with 500 credits a month on Solo, 1,000 per user on Professional, and unlimited credits on Business, so the cost stays transparent.

## Which Platform Delivers Better Outreach Results?

Pin delivers 5x better outreach response rates than industry averages across email, LinkedIn, and SMS. Noon AI doesn't publish response rate benchmarks, making a direct comparison impossible on this metric. For context, the average cold email response rate hovers around 5% and LinkedIn outreach averages roughly 10% - benchmarks consistently cited across email marketing and sales research. Pin's multi-channel performance sits well above both baselines.

Those industry-leading numbers aren't accidental. The system sends personalized, multi-channel sequences that feel human because they're built on Pin's data about what messaging works for which candidates and roles. The channel mix itself moves the needle. Across a sample of 1,000,000+ outreach sequences in Pin's index, recruiters who paired email with LinkedIn saw a 45% higher candidate response rate than email-only campaigns. Switch from generic cold outreach to Pin's automated sequences and candidates respond because the messages read as relevant, not robotic.
